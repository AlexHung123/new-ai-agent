# Outsourcing security

Outsourcing is an arrangement with an organisation outside the Government to provide services the B/D could undertake itself. External service providers engaged in government work shall observe and comply with the B/D’s departmental IT security policy and other government information-security requirements (G3 s.17.1(a)). See [[13-outsourcing]] and [[compare-documents]].

## Residual B/D ownership

Although development, implementation, and/or maintenance of an information system can be outsourced, **the overall responsibility of the information system remains under B/Ds** (G3 s.17.1(a)). The same sentence is restated for cloud: overall **accountability** remains under the B/Ds (G3 s.17.3(a)). Security roles of provider, B/D, and end users should be defined, agreed, and documented. B/Ds shall identify and assess risks to government data and operations; classify all data to be handled; document and implement security measures, service levels, and management requirements commensurate with classification and business needs.

## No unsupervised / no remote day-to-day production access

B/Ds **shall not** allow external service providers **access rights to government information systems and data in a production environment**. If access is deemed necessary (e.g. system maintenance and support), it **must be closely supervised** by authorised personnel **under a controlled environment**. **Remote access to production systems and data by external service providers for carrying out day-to-day management and operation shall be strictly prohibited** (G3 s.17.1(a)).

External consultants, contractors, outsourced staff, and temporary staff shall not have access until appropriate controls are implemented and a contract is signed defining the terms (G3 s.17.1(b)).

## NDA / SLA and contract hygiene (G3 s.17.1(b))

The outsourcing contract should require provider staff to sign a **non-disclosure agreement** where access to classified information is required, and should include **service level agreements** (expected performance of each security control, measurable outcomes, remedies and response for non-compliance, liability, reliability, response times). Providers shall not transfer or disclose classified government data to third parties without the Government’s prior written consent; special third-party disclosure requests shall be redirected to the B/D. Contracts should include procedures for securely erasing government data on all platforms with written confirmation, plus escalation for problem resolution and incident response. Indemnity clauses should protect the Government from disruption or malpractice (G3 s.17.1(c)).

B/Ds should contractually reserve audit and compliance-monitoring rights, or require periodic satisfactory security audit/certification reports (G3 s.17.2(a)). At expiry or termination, all government data in external services or facilities **shall** be cleared or destroyed according to classification; provider staff **shall** return all government assets (G3 s.17.2(b)).

## Cloud shared responsibility still B/D accountable

Shared responsibilities divide security and management between the Cloud Service Provider (public or private) and the cloud customer. Before signing, B/Ds **shall** ensure those responsibilities are clearly defined, documented, and understood; review terms of service, data-protection policy, and CSP security measures. After signing, review regularly that the CSP is adhering to its part. **This does not move overall accountability off the B/D** (G3 s.17.3(a)). Practice Guide for Cloud Computing Security is on ITG InfoStation.

The information or system owner should know **where** data is hosted and ensure measures comply with relevant security requirements and local laws (G3 s.17.1(a)).
