## Management direction (G3 s.8 intro)

B/Ds **shall** define and enforce their IT security policies to provide management direction and support for protecting information systems and assets in accordance with business needs and security requirements.

## Departmental IT security policy (G3 s.8.1(a))

IT security policy **shall** set the **minimum standards** of a security specification and state what is of paramount importance to the organisation. It **shall** be treated as the **basic rules to be observed as mandatory**, while other desirable measures can still enhance security.

B/Ds **shall** establish the departmental IT security policy **based on the Baseline IT Security Policy [S17]** as the basis for development.

The policy **shall** cover:

- proper use of information systems, data assets, network resources, IT services, and facilities; and
- procedures to **prevent and respond** to security incidents.

Drafting **shall** consider:

- the B/D’s own security requirements;
- prevailing government IT security requirements as specified in G3 s.2.3 (SR, S17, G3, practice guides);
- the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance;
- the Code on Access to Information;
- information on record management in the Manual of Office Practice.

Drafting **shall additionally** consider:

- goals and direction of the Government of the HKSAR;
- existing policies, rules, regulations, and laws of the Government of the HKSAR;
- the B/D’s own requirements and needs;
- implementation, distribution, and enforcement issues.

B/Ds **should** set up procedures to provide prompt assistance in investigative matters relating to breaches of security and policy implementation. Establishing a Departmental Information Security Incident Response Team ([[isirt|ISIRT]]) and a security incident response plan can improve the effectiveness of the policy.

## Evaluation and periodic review (G3 s.8.1(b))

Review of information security policies, standards, guidelines, and procedures **shall** be conducted **periodically**. Results and proposed changes **shall** be evaluated and endorsed by related parties so that necessary requirements are incorporated. B/Ds **may** hire external qualified IT security auditors or consultants to review or assist in developing the documents, to improve quality and completeness.

Documents without ongoing support become unattended and outdated: some issues diminish while new ones appear. Frequent reviews help the policy meet latest requirements and cope with technological change.

## Communication with users (G3 s.8.1(c))

B/Ds **shall** promulgate their own IT Security Policy. A delivery mechanism **shall** be established so the policy is easy to access and available to all staff, functional groups, and management. B/Ds **shall** ensure they are fully aware of the policy so they can carry out their duties and meet government security requirements.

No policy shall be considered implemented unless users or related parties have **commitment and communication**. B/Ds **should** make sure that users and related parties:

- are informed of the policy by **briefing or orientation** when they newly join;
- are invited to participate in developing policy proposals;
- are **trained** with the skills needed to follow the policy;
- are **periodically reminded** and refreshed on security threats or issues;
- are provided with policy guidance in **manageable units**.

To help an end user understand IT security responsibilities, B/Ds **should** develop a departmental **end-user instruction** document that highlights end-user-related requirements in a simple instruction format. A sample template is [[annex-end-user|Appendix A — Sample IT Security End User Instructions]].
