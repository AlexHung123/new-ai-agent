## General management (G3 s.7 intro, s.7.1)

The Head of B/D **shall** put in place effective security arrangements so that government information systems and assets are safeguarded and IT services are delivered securely.

### Roles and responsibilities (G3 s.7.1(a))

B/Ds **shall** apply core security principles and best practices on **checks and balances**. Information security **shall** be considered in **all stages** of project management, regardless of project type.

Regardless of **sources of funding**, B/Ds **shall** ensure all of their information systems — including infrastructure facilities and departmental shared IT services — are properly protected in accordance with their risk levels. [[classified-protection|Classified protection]] (G3 s.7.2(b)) **shall** be adopted. Protection **shall** be responsive and adaptive to changing environments and technology.

B/Ds **shall** define their own departmental IT security management framework with reference to G3 s.5.2 ([[01-organisation]]). A senior and key person **should** be assigned responsibility for ensuring that appropriate policies and procedures are developed and applied, and that necessary checks and balances on administration and operation are in place. Assignments **should** refer to that framework, policies, and procedures.

Staff with assigned responsibilities **may** delegate security tasks, but they remain **ultimately accountable** for adequate measures. They **should** ensure delegates have the capability, knowledge, experience, and seniority, and **should** check that delegated tasks have been performed properly. **Details of delegation shall be documented and periodically reviewed.**

### Segregation of duties (G3 s.7.1(b))

[[segregation-of-duties|Segregation of duties]] divides the steps in a function among different individuals so that a single person cannot subvert a process. There **shall** be sufficient segregation, with roles clearly defined, to minimise the chance that one individual has authority to execute and control **all** security functions and/or crucial operations of a system.

Where segregation is not practicable (limited staff or technical limitations), **compensating controls should** be put in place for equivalent safeguards — for example, appropriate **logging** of critical operations, plus random inspection and/or regular review of the log by an appropriate level of authority.

### Budgeting (G3 s.7.1(c))

B/Ds **shall** control the budget to ensure sufficient resource allocation for necessary security safeguards. Management **should** develop information security budget plans, projections, and resource allocations based on short-term and long-term objectives. Resources **should** be allocated according to systems’ risk levels.

### Rights for information examination (G3 s.7.1(d))

B/Ds **shall** reserve the right to examine all information stored in or transmitted by government information systems — including emails, file directories, and access records to discussion boards, newsgroups, and websites — **in compliance with the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance**. Examination helps assure compliance with internal policies, supports internal investigations, and facilitates security management.

## Security risk management (G3 s.7.2)

### Risk-based approach (G3 s.7.2(a))

B/Ds **shall** adopt a risk-based approach to information security to ensure CIA of information assets and all other security aspects of information systems in a changing environment. Simple measures **should** mitigate and control potential human and/or operation risks to an acceptable, manageable level. B/Ds **shall** consider best practices for possible adoption given their business and operation environments.

### Classified protection of IT security (G3 s.7.2(b))

To protect systems with controls commensurate with risk, B/Ds **shall** adopt classified protection by assessing the classifications of **all** their information systems — including infrastructure facilities and departmental shared IT services, **regardless of funding** — and implementing **tiered** controls: [[classified-protection|Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3]].

B/Ds **shall** adopt **all mandatory security requirements in this document** for ordinary (Tier 1) information systems, and **additionally** the more stringent requirements in [[annex-classified-protection|Appendix C]] for Tier 2 and Tier 3. Classifications **shall** align with business objectives across the **entire life cycle**.

**Tier 2** systems are those crucial to operations of the Government or society, whose failure or disruption will result in a serious impact on government operations or may cause public turmoil and catastrophes. When determining criticality, B/Ds **should** consider data classification and consequences of service disruption, including:

- Defence/security risks (e.g. harm to human lives or properties, personal privacy, inability to carry out statutory duties and maintain law and order)
- Financial implications (e.g. potential to reduce economic growth, financial loss of the Government)
- Government image (e.g. effect on government reputation, public confidence)
- Interdependency (e.g. degradation of one system disrupting another)
- Other aspects that apply to their systems

Assessment **should** address **scope** (number of users affected), **severity** (consequences of disruption or destruction), **downtime tolerance** (the point at which disruption has a serious impact), and the **largest potential business impact** from failure or disruption.

**Tier 3** systems are Tier 2 systems **directly related** to provision of an **essential service** (critical to the functioning and security of a society and its economy) whose disruption or destruction may cause serious harm to the economy, people’s livelihood, public safety, etc.

B/Ds **shall** determine classifications **during the project initiation stage**. Assessment **shall** make reference to [[annex-classification|Appendix B]]. Assessment details of **all** information systems **shall** be properly documented. Classifications **shall** be endorsed by the **Head of B/D** or an **explicitly delegated officer at directorate level**.

### IT security risk management framework (G3 s.7.2(c))

B/Ds **should** adopt the following framework so that security risk assessments are performed and monitored in a structured way, using risk registers:

| Step | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Departmental context establishment | Establish the B/D’s IT security risk-management context, including **risk appetite and tolerance** |
| Risk assessment | Perform [[security-risk-assessment|security risk assessments]] specified in G3 s.20.2(a) for **all** information systems. Identify risks by sources (e.g. vulnerabilities, threats) and events (e.g. incident scenarios); determine level by impact and likelihood; prioritise for treatment; document in **system** risk registers |
| Risk treatment | Determine treatment (e.g. reduction, avoidance, transfer, acceptance) for each risk so it falls within appetite; document in system risk registers |
| Correlation, aggregation, normalisation | Consolidate system registers into a **departmental** IT security risk register, in the departmental context, to facilitate monitoring and communication |
| Monitoring and reporting | Monitor departmental IT security risks and corresponding treatment, and **report them to [[ditso\|DITSO]]** and other relevant parties |

Further detail: *Practice Guide for IT Security Risk Management* (ITG InfoStation). Do not treat the practice-guide title as a wiki page.
