# Appendix A — sample end-user instructions

G3 Appendix A is a **sample** to help end users understand their IT-security responsibilities. B/Ds should **customise** it from their departmental IT security policy and computer environment, distribute it to existing staff and to new staff at first entry, and remind staff regularly. It is **not** a replacement for departmental security documents, the Security Regulations, Baseline IT Security Policy [S17], or G3. Users must still follow those in full.

The sample names a [[ditso|DITSO]], states that security is a **personal responsibility**, and that each user is accountable for all of his or her activities. Observe SR and S17 for classified or personal information. Apply **need-to-know**. Consult the Departmental Security Officer if in doubt about authorised access. Protect computers and storage under custody; report suspicious activity to a help desk (office hours) or named after-hours contacts. Failure to comply may result in disciplinary proceedings.

The DOs/DON’Ts lists are **not exhaustive**. Distinctive items below; full departmental policy, SR and S17 still apply.

## DOs (sample)

- Mark classification (e.g. `[RESTRICTED]` in the email subject).
- **Encrypt all stored classified information.** Classified information **should** be encrypted in transit on any network; CONFIDENTIAL / RESTRICTED **shall** be encrypted on **un-trusted** networks ([[classified-information]]).
- CONFIDENTIAL email **within Government**: use **CMS, CMSG, MCMS, and approved CMMP sub-systems** (sample wording: Confidential Mail System, Confidential Messaging System, Mobile Confidential Mail Services, approved CMMP sub-systems).
- Check recipient addresses; watch mailbox rules, missing inbound mail, and outbound mail you did not write; verify authenticity of address, URL and spelling.
- Back up critical data; keep an **offline** copy with sufficient protection.
- Minimise classified or personal data on mobile phones; supervise them in use; store them commensurate with classification when not in use.
- Password-protect, log off or lock equipment when unattended; privacy-screen against eavesdropping; grant access on need-to-know.
- Passwords per departmental policy (sample: at least eight characters with upper-case, lower-case, numbers and special characters; change periodically; change immediately and report if compromised) — [[password-management]].
- **Unique** complex password per system (official email ≠ personal email); **MFA** for online accounts if available.
- Patch; clear cache; keep malware signatures current; scan email, downloads and removable media.
- Ignore or delete spam; beware phishing. (Footnotes **8** and **9** on spam and phishing are **not** in this conversion — do not guess the bodies.)
- Encrypt wireless/mobile traffic; password-protect devices; disable wireless, NFC, Bluetooth and infrared when unused; disable automatic Wi-Fi in public places.
- Follow the Guiding Principles on the Use of Internet Services when using Government Internet. (Footnote **10** is **not** in this conversion.)

## DON’Ts (sample)

- **Don’t** store classified information on **privately owned** mobile devices, removable media or IoT devices.
- Don’t leave workstations unattended without physical access control.
- Don’t write passwords near the desk, use guessable or personal information as a password, or **share** passwords.
- Don’t disclose information about yourself, your system or your department to unauthorised persons.
- **Don’t connect your own device** to the government internal information system or network.
- Don’t connect workstations to external networks by dial-up, wireless or broadband (the sample is stricter than G3 s.15.1(b), which allows simultaneous internal + external **only** with DITSO approval — customise against the live departmental rule).
- Don’t open attachments or click links in unsolicited or suspicious messages (email, IM, SMS).
- Don’t download from unconfirmed websites.
- Don’t use a government email address to register **non-work** online services; don’t reuse the government-email password elsewhere.
- **Don’t use any private email service for official communication**, especially with the public or external parties in an official capacity.
- Don’t install software without approval; don’t disable endpoint protection; don’t access files from unknown removable media; don’t run unauthorised software without prior approval from the designated officer.
