# Incremental approach

The booklet’s official stance on first-year Plans: do not wait for a complete, single, sophisticated document. Chapter: [[05-follow-up]].

## Several training sections (s.3.2)

Some departments have a number of sections responsible for training. Ideally they produce a **master** [[departmental-training-plan]]. If that is impracticable at first — especially if they are not used to drawing up such plans — produce a number of **separate** plans in the first or second year, then move toward a consolidated approach.

## First-year Plans (s.6)

Most departments are not yet experienced. A [[departmental-training-committee]] already formed is an example of initial steps already being followed. Departments with only a rudimentary training function have catching up to do.

During the first year:

- not every Plan can be expected to achieve perfection
- some information may be missing at the start of the year (example: **posting decisions** that will affect training)
- some departments may have little to say under some headings in [[04-contents]]

Once a yearly Plan is a natural part of the department’s process, forward planning becomes complete and sophisticated. The project should be approached **in good faith**, with care and thought for the future.

Amendments during the year are expected anyway (Guide s.5). Incremental drafting and in-year amendment are the same stance.
