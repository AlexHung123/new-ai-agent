# Synthesis — what this booklet is actually regulating

This is a **how-to for a yearly working document**, not a regulation and not a course catalogue. CSTC wants every department to write, before the financial year, a Plan of the training it will organise or buy, then send copies to [[csb]] and [[cstc]]. The wiki’s job is to make that process readable: where needs come from, what is filtered out, and what the Plan must still contain.

## 1. The Plan is a working document of actual needs

Guide s.1: a practical document of the **full scope** of training activities for the coming year, in-house or with outside resources. Guide s.3 is the method sentence: formulate according to **actual training needs**. Last year’s list is a useful starting point and nothing more. Using last year’s prospectus as this year’s Plan is the failure mode the booklet is written against.

## 2. Demands are gathered, then cut

[[02-establishing-needs]] collects from the corporate/business plan, from a survey if in-house expertise is missing, and from [[performance-appraisal-training]]. [[03-matching]] then says it is **unlikely** that all stated needs will be accommodated. The Plan is not the sum of those demands. The cut is against **funds and manpower** and against [[departmental-training-policy]] (entitlement floors and ceilings, category priorities, whether the Training Committee outranks supervisor–subordinate agreements, and the share of departmental expenses).

## 3. Informal development is in the booklet and out of the Plan

After appraisal, two layers exist. The standard form (Appendix 1) is the **formal** layer and feeds the Plan. Non-classroom learning is **as important** and **does not feature** in the Plan because it is carried out individually (Guide p.2 / s.3.1). That is why [[informal-development]] and [[classroom-training]] are separate pages. Sharing after a course **does** appear later, as an “other training-related activity” in the Plan (Guide s.4 item 7) — organised sharing is a departmental effort; individual non-classroom development is not.

## 4. CSTC’s 2-day / 4-day figures are a view, not a ceiling

[[training-entitlement]] may differ by grade, rank, and department. CSTC’s view: MPS Point 16 or below, **at least 2 days** a year; MPS Point 17 or above, **at least 4 days**. Departments should also set **maximum** ceilings. The booklet does not make these Civil Service Regulations. See [[compare-entitlement]].

## 5. Two copies, two audiences

The same Plan is for the department (organise work around releases; assess itself at year end) and for the centre ([[cstc]] allocates central courses from the copies it receives). Follow-up (Guide s.5) is: endorse via the [[departmental-training-committee]] or HRM Advisory Team, send one copy to [[csb]] and one to CSTC, notify participants and superiors even without exact dates, and report amendments to the Committee and/or [[branch-rmu]].

## 6. Incremental is official policy, not an apology

Most departments “are not yet experienced”. A Training Committee already formed is enough of a start. Rudimentary training functions have catching up to do. First-year Plans may omit headings, and posting decisions may still be open. Several training sections may file **separate** plans in year one or two, then move to a **master** Plan (Guide s.3.2 and s.6). Perfection is not the first-year test; good faith is.

## 7. This conversion is the booklet, not the rest of the series

Five companion titles are named and **not in this file**: Formulating Departmental Training Policies; Induction Training; Training Evaluation; Training Needs Analysis; Roles and Functions of Training Personnel in Departments. The body also cites *Formulating Departmental Training Policies and Strategies* (wording differs from the series list). SSCC appears once as a course provider; this file does not expand the abbreviation. The Appendix example is dated **August 1994**. “Training fund status” is as printed. For a live departmental Plan, use current bureau/college instructions — not this wiki.
