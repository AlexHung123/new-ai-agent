# Establishing training needs

Guide p.2 / s.3–s.3.1. First half of “how the Plan is prepared”. Matching those needs to resources and policy: [[03-matching]]. Concept page: [[training-needs-analysis]]. Sources compared: [[compare-needs-sources]].

## Method sentence (s.3)

The heading **How is the plan prepared** is in the table of contents and missing in the body. This paragraph sits where that heading belongs:

A training plan should be formulated according to the **actual training needs** of a department. The previous year’s activities may be a useful starting point, but that is all — the department’s needs may have changed considerably.

## Companion booklet

CSTC’s *A Guide to Training Needs Analysis* gives a practical and detailed account of how to establish training needs. That booklet is **not in this compilation**. This chapter records only the three areas this guide lists.

## Three areas (s.3.1)

### (a) Corporate / business strategies

Once the department’s management team has established its future corporate/business plan, it may consult human resource management specialists to evaluate manpower implications. Those specialists may sit in the HRM team or the [[departmental-training-committee]].

After studying the corporate plan they should advise on the staff needed to implement it:

- competencies / skills / knowledge required
- experience levels
- numbers
- when they will be needed

If the required competency, skill, or knowledge is **not currently available**, the training and development implications must be addressed. This is particularly important if the future business strategy involves activities **not previously undertaken** by the department.

### (b) Outside consultation

If the department does not have the necessary in-house HRM or training expertise, it may need a **formal training needs survey** to assess manpower implications. It may seek professional advice from [[cstc]]. If CSTC does not have the resources to help **and funds are available**, the department may appoint a management consultancy firm.

### (c) Performance appraisal reports

Training needs are often established through performance appraisal, when the type and timing of training the appraisee should undertake in the following year is agreed. Detail: [[performance-appraisal-training]].

The [[training-section]] should supply both appraisers and appraisees with a list of:

- courses run by the department in the past
- CSTC courses
- SSCC courses (abbreviation not expanded in this file)
- other courses available to the department

Two follow-up routes after the agreed training:

| Route | Who acts |
| --- | --- |
| i) Direct to Training Section | The Section notes the requirements and consolidates them into overall needs for the coming year. It should design a **standard form** for appraisers to complete after the interview. Sample: [[annex-training-agreement]] |
| ii) Via operational head | Completed forms are first vetted by the operational head, who considers the needs, determines priorities, and submits views to the Training Section |

## Formal versus informal

Needs agreed and entered on the standard form are the **formal** aspect of training for the appraisee. There is also an **informal** aspect of non-classroom learning and development. That aspect is as important as the formal one. It **does not feature** in the departmental training plan because it is carried out individually. See [[informal-development]] and [[compare-formal-vs-informal]].
