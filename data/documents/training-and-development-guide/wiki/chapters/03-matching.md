# Matching needs with resources and policies

Guide p.3 / s.3.2. Second half of “how the Plan is prepared”. Gathering needs: [[02-establishing-needs]]. What the finished Plan contains: [[04-contents]].

## (a) Resources

Because of limits on **funds and manpower**, it is unlikely that all stated training needs — from appraisal reports or needs surveys — will be accommodated.

The [[departmental-training-plan]] should **not** be regarded simply as a sum total of the training demands gathered from various sources. The [[training-section]] has to match those needs against:

- its capacity and resources; and
- [[departmental-training-policy|departmental training policies]].

## (b) Departmental training policies

These set out guidelines on how the training function of a particular department should be managed. CSTC’s *Formulating Departmental Training Policies and Strategies* describes the parameters (that booklet is **not in this compilation**; the series list titles it *Formulating Departmental Training Policies*).

Four parameters this guide calls particularly important:

| | Parameter |
| --- | --- |
| i) | [[training-entitlement\|Individual training entitlement]] — days each year an individual may spend on training or training-related activities. May differ by grade, rank, and department. CSTC view and ceilings: [[compare-entitlement]] |
| ii) | Training priorities for induction, professional/technical, management, language, communication, and information technology (including PCs). Course lists in the Plan use a related split: [[compare-course-categories]] |
| iii) | Whether recommendations of the [[departmental-training-committee]] **take precedence** over training needs agreed between supervisors and their subordinates |
| iv) | The **proportion of departmental expenses** to be spent on training and development |

## (c) Formulating the Training Plan

Once those factors are taken into account, the Training Section can formulate the Plan. In doing so it may need to:

1. consult and work with the departmental training committee on funding, priorities, and so on;
2. seek the advice of [[cstc]], particularly for training in **management, language, and communication**, and the advice of other professional/training institutes on **technical** training;
3. liaise with the **personnel section** to ascertain the number of intakes, so as to plan induction training.

## Several training sections

Some departments have a number of sections responsible for training. Ideally they work together to produce a **master** Training Plan. That may at first prove impracticable, especially if they are not used to drawing up such plans. The project may then be approached [[incremental-approach|incrementally]]: a number of **separate** plans in the first or second year, moving toward a consolidated approach.
