## Government Information Security Management Framework (S17 s.5.1)

To coordinate and promote IT security in the Government, an Information Security Management Framework of **five parties** has been established: [[ismc|ISMC]], [[itswg|ITSWG]], [[giro|GIRO]], [[govcert|GovCERT.HK]], and Bureaux/Departments. The diagram captioned “Government Information Security Management Framework” is an **image** in this conversion. Roles below are from the surrounding text, not reconstructed boxes.

### ISMC (S17 s.5.1.1)

The Information Security Management Committee was established in **April 2000** to oversee IT security across the Government. It meets regularly to: review and endorse changes to government IT security related regulations, policies, and guidelines; define specific roles and responsibilities relating to IT security; and provide guidance and assistance to B/Ds in enforcement **through ITSWG**.

Core members: Digital Policy Office (DPO) and Security Bureau (SB). Representatives from other B/Ds are co-opted as needed. DPO assists in reviewing and clarifying documents B/Ds submit as required in this document.

### ITSWG (S17 s.5.1.2)

The IT Security Working Group, established in **May 2000**, is the **executive arm of ISMC** for promulgation and compliance monitoring. Responsibilities: coordinate guidance and assistance to B/Ds on enforcement; **monitor compliance with the Baseline IT Security Policy** at B/Ds; define and review IT security related regulations, policies, and guidelines; promote IT security awareness in the Government.

Core members: DPO, SB, Hong Kong Police Force (HKPF), and Chief Secretary for Administration’s Office (CSO). Other B/Ds co-opted as needed.

### GIRO (S17 s.5.1.3)

Each B/D **shall** establish an Information Security Incident Response Team ([[isirt|ISIRT]]). The Government Information Security Incident Response Office provides **central coordination and support** to those ISIRTs. The GIRO Standing Office is GIRO’s executive arm.

Major functions: maintain a central inventory and oversee handling of all government information security incidents; prepare periodic statistics; coordinate handling of **multiple-point** security attacks (simultaneous attacks on different government systems); enable experience sharing among B/D ISIRTs.

Core members: DPO, SB, HKPF.

### GovCERT.HK (S17 s.5.1.4)

The Government Computer Emergency Response Team Hong Kong was established in **April 2015**. It collaborates with the GIRO Standing Office on information and cyber security incidents in the Government, and with the CERT community on incident information, threat intelligence, and best practices.

Major functions: disseminate security alerts on impending and actual threats to B/Ds; act as a bridge between the Hong Kong Computer Emergency Response Team Coordination Centre (HKCERT) and other computer security incident response teams (CSIRTs) in handling cyber security incidents.

### Bureaux/Departments (S17 s.5.1.5)

B/Ds **shall** be responsible for the security protection of their information assets and information systems. Departmental roles: S17 s.5.2.

## Departmental IT security organisation (S17 s.5.2)

For sufficient [[segregation-of-duties|segregation of duties]], **multiple roles should not be assigned to an individual** unless there is a resource limitation. ### DITSO (S17 s.5.2.1)

The Head of B/D **shall** appoint an officer at **D3 or above** from senior management as Departmental IT Security Officer ([[ditso|DITSO]]), responsible for IT security. The DITSO shall participate in overall steering, and shall understand the B/D’s priorities, the importance of its systems and data, and the security level that shall be achieved.

If the B/D has **no directorate officer at D3 or above**, the **highest-rank directorate officer** shall assume DITSO, to uphold accountability in IT security.

SB and DPO provide training; DITSOs **shall** attend the designated training. Duties **shall** be clearly defined and include (non-exhaustive): an information-protection programme for all staff; a security governance process (evaluate, direct, monitor, communicate); regular senior-management discussion of IT security and resources; lead policies, standards, procedures, and guidelines; oversee effectiveness through every stage of IT operations; monitor compliance including satisfactory completion of security audits; oversee awareness and training; coordinate with other B/Ds; oversee IT risk management (security risk assessments, privacy impact assessments, rectifications, evolving risk, regulation, technology, and system criticality); oversee threat detection, monitoring, and intelligence, including dissemination of [[giro|GIRO]] alerts; initiate investigation and rectification on breach and coordinate incident reports to the Director of Bureau.

### Information Security Steering Committee (S17 s.5.2.2)

Senior management **should** consider setting up an information security steering committee that **reports to DITSO**, or include information security as a regular item in management meetings. Committee duties: leadership in promoting and prioritising IT security; direct and enforce development of security measures; provide resources; foster orderly and secure functioning of all systems; ensure participation and accountability at all levels; foster a culture of awareness and accountability; align IT security strategies with business objectives.

If B/Ds choose not to set up a committee, DITSO **should** undertake the committee’s responsibilities.

### DSO (S17 s.5.2.3)

The Head of B/D will designate a Departmental Security Officer ([[dso|DSO]]) to perform departmental security related duties. The DSO takes the role of an executive to: discharge responsibilities for **all aspects of security** for the B/D; and advise on the set-up and review of the security policy. The DSO **may** take on the role of DITSO. If someone else is appointed, the DITSO **shall** collaborate with the DSO.

### ISIRT Commander (S17 s.5.2.4)

The ISIRT is the central focal point for coordinating the handling of information security incidents within the B/D. The Head of B/D **should** designate an officer from senior management as [[isirt|ISIRT]] Commander. The Commander should have the authority to appoint core team members. Responsibilities: overall supervision and coordination of incident handling for all systems in the B/D; decisions on damage containment, system recovery, engagement of external parties, and service-resumption logistics; trigger departmental disaster-recovery procedure where appropriate; management endorsement of resources and of the line-to-take for publicity; collaborate with GIRO in reporting incidents for central recording and follow-up; facilitate experience sharing within the B/D.

### IT Security Management Unit (S17 s.5.2.5)

B/Ds **shall** establish an IT security management unit which **reports to DITSO** and assists DITSO in discharging duties. Size and composition may vary with business and operational needs. Responsibilities: assist DITSO on governance and on IT security management for all IT operations; assist in developing the overall IT security strategy and roadmap (policies, baselines, standards, directives); coordinate awareness and training; coordinate implementation of IT security initiatives and monitor processes for effectiveness and compliance; facilitate threat and risk management and support DR/BCP functions relating to IT security; coordinate incident investigation and rectification; liaise with other B/Ds; any other duties as directed by DITSO.

## Other roles (S17 s.5.3)

**IT Security Administrators** shall provide security and risk-management support: identify and mitigate vulnerabilities; assist in [[patch-management]]; implement access controls and manage privileges; maintain and review audit logs (they **should not tamper with or change** any audit log); monitor threat-intelligence sources; operate IDS/IPS and similar tools. The IT Security Administrator **should not** be the same person as the System Administrator ([[segregation-of-duties]]). B/Ds **may** appoint an IT Security Auditor to audit the Administrators.

**Information Owners** shall be the collators and owners of information stored in information systems. Primary responsibility: determine data classifications, authorised data usage, and corresponding security requirements ([[information-owner]]).

**LAN/System Administrators** shall be responsible for day-to-day administration, operation, and configuration of computer systems and networks; Internet System Administrators for Internet-facing systems. They implement security mechanisms and controls according to procedures/guidelines established by the DITSO.

**Application Development & Maintenance Team** shall produce quality systems with quality procedures, techniques, and tools. They liaise with the Information Owner to define and implement system security requirements during development and maintenance, and ensure those quality procedures are used to produce secure systems.

**Users** of information systems shall be the staff authorised to access and use the information. Users shall be accountable for all their activities. Responsibilities: attend awareness and training directed by the B/D; know, understand, follow, and apply available security mechanisms to the maximum extent possible; prevent leakage and unauthorised access to information under their custody; safekeep computing and storage devices and protect them from unauthorised access or malicious attack with their best effort.
