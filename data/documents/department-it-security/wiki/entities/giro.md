# Government Information Security Incident Response Officer

[[giro|GIRO]] is the Government Information Security Incident Response Officer (DITSP s.4.2). In this booklet GIRO is the government-wide contact for **central recording and coordination** of information security incidents. DITSP does not describe GIRO’s office structure; it only sets when CSB must notify. See [[incident-handling]], [[isirt]], and [[08-incident]].

## When CSB notifies GIRO (DITSP s.21.4)

If the immediate escalation point cannot be reached, the next party should be contacted within the defined timeframe.

**Serious or public-aware incidents** (service interruptions or hacking that may be noticed by the public, and/or that occur in critical system components): the Division IRM notifies CSB [[itmu-security-team|ITMU]], the [[dso|DSO]], and GIRO within **60 minutes** of the incident report, after Division ISIRTC approval. From then on, every 30 minutes, the IRM or an authorised officer reports status to the Division ISIRTC, CSB ITMU, and GIRO. After system recovery, the Division ISIRTC informs CSB ITMU and GIRO by **email** so the closed case is recorded.

The [[isirt|ISIRT]] should also report to GIRO when an incident hits any of these criteria (not limited to them):

- human life and safety;
- information systems that provide a public service directly and whose failure interrupts that service (for example a denial-of-service attack on a Government Internet web site);
- information systems that handle sensitive data (for example CMS);
- information systems that support mission-critical operation;
- an information system that would suffer a highly undesirable impact (for example Government Internet web-site defacement).

If a suspected computer crime is reported to [[tcd]], the ISIRT must also notify GIRO for central recording and coordination support.

**Other incidents** (interruptions and minor breaches that may not be public): after recovery, and subject to the Division ISIRTC’s decision, the IRM reports the incident to CSB ITMU and GIRO by email for recording. Those reports are not on the 60-minute serious-incident clock.
