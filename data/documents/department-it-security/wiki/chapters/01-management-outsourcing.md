# Outsourcing security

DITSP ss.6.2, 14.1.7, and 14.2: third parties carry the **same security duties as staff**; residual ownership stays with CSB. Hub: [[01-management]]. Concept page: [[outsourcing-security]].

## Policy (DITSP s.6.2)

Tagged **[M – IS Owner]**. Outsourcing or third-party providers **shall observe and comply** with this DITSP and other Government information-security requirements (s.6.2.1). CSB shall monitor and review with those providers so that security operations are managed properly (s.6.2.2).

External consultants, contractors, outsourced staff, and temporary staff engaged on Government work **shall be subject to the same information security requirements, and have the same information security responsibilities, as Government staff** (s.6.2.3).

[[information-system-owner|IS Owners]] remain accountable for CIA of outsourced systems under s.6.1.7 ([[01-management]]).

## Security requirements in contracts (DITSP s.14.1.7)

IS Owners shall ensure controls administer access by external consultants, contractors, and temporary staff. Security requirements from third-party access or internal controls **shall** be reflected in the third-party contract or other agreement. Where confidentiality needs extra protection, a **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)** should be established.

Access to CSB information and systems **shall not be provided** until appropriate controls are in place **and** a contract has been signed defining the terms of connection or access.

As a basic principle, the security policies, procedures, and checks and balances that apply to in-house staff **should also apply** to all external consultants, contractors, and temporary staff involved.

## Outsourcing process (DITSP s.14.2)

When a system is outsourced, security management processes must protect the data and mitigate risks of the outsourced project or service.

The **project owner** of the outsourced service should define the security requirements of the systems to be outsourced, and should seek advice from the [[itmu-security-team|CSB ITMU Security Team]] on the latest IT security requirements. Those requirements should form the basis of tendering and of **performance metrics**.

**Unauthorised materials (v1.1, May 2008).** Project owners should check and remind their outsourcing contractors that **any unauthorised materials are removed from their government computers**.

The contract should require third-party staff to sign an **NDA** to protect sensitive data. It should also include **Service Level Agreements (SLA)** that define expected performance for each required security control, describe measurable outcomes, and identify remedies and response requirements for non-compliance. It should include an **escalation** process for problem resolution and incident response so incidents follow a pre-defined path and impact on CSB is minimised.

Project owners should ensure providers employ adequate controls under DITSP and other Government regulations throughout the engagement. Providers are subject to the **same requirements and the same responsibilities as Government staff** (repeats s.6.2.3).

CSB should monitor security-control compliance of providers and users actively and periodically. CSB **must reserve the right to audit** responsibilities defined in the SLA, to have those audits carried out by an independent third party, and to enumerate the statutory rights of auditors. CSB should also ensure the provider’s contingency plan and backup process are adequate ([[01-management-contingency]], [[disaster-recovery]]).

Security roles of the provider, CSB, and end users should be clearly defined and documented. **Although a system can be outsourced, overall responsibility for it remains under CSB.**
