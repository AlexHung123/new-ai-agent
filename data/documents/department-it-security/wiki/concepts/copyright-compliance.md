# Copyright compliance

Copyright-law restrictions must be respected at all times. Only approved software and hardware with **purchased licences** may be set up and installed, following the licence agreements. Unauthorised copying, modification, or unlicensed use is strictly prohibited (DITSP s.17.8). Software-usage guidelines (s.19.5.1) point at **Appendix D**, added in version 1.2 (July 2008). Full text of the appendix: [[annex-copyright]]. Patching of licensed systems: [[patch-management]]. Unauthorised software on a Government system also needs [[ditso|Division DITSO]] approval under s.11.5.2.

The copyright of information systems or software **developed by CSB is owned by the Government**. Unauthorised alteration may add a security threat or cause malfunctions (s.17.8).

## Unauthorised software ban (Appendix D)

Under the prevailing [[ogcio]] circular, **unauthorised software** means software not licensed for use, **or** software licensed for use but **not authorised by the bureau/department**. Installation of unauthorised or illegal (pirated) software on office workstations is **strictly prohibited**. Freeware/shareware of any type obtained from the Internet (including screen savers, wallpaper, pictures, images) should not be downloaded to run on office computers without the copyright owner’s express permission **and** Bureau approval.

It is the **personal responsibility** of colleagues to ensure their workstations contain no unlicensed or unauthorised software. Do not install anything without ITMU technical advice and endorsement. Divisions should seek ITMU advice **before procuring** hardware or software. Personally licensed software on an office workstation is also not allowed. Where a division has a justified need, it makes a case to ITMU; ITMU considers it case by case and may seek OGCIO advice.

s.19.5.1: all software should be acquired from an authorised dealer/supplier. Illegal copies shall not be installed or run. Public-domain software and freeware have a higher chance of malware; avoid them; if necessary, install only with Division DITSO or [[itmu-security-team]] approval. Close-source freeware: long history (at least two years) and good track record. Open-source: carefully inspect. Freeware licences may not cover business use.

File-sharing / P2P is a separate control ([[annex-file-sharing]]); do not share unauthorised copyright materials.

## SAM / SMS (s.17.8–17.9)

Security-control procedures should ensure compliance with software licences, purchase agreements, and copyright legislation. An inventory of software on each server should be audited against licence agreements regularly (e.g. once a year). Licences, manuals, and procurement documentation should be stored securely; keep the inventory current. When upgrades are purchased, the old version may have to be disposed of under the purchase agreement.

CSB has deployed **Microsoft Systems Management Server (SMS)** to automate software deployment, inventory scanning, and metering: deploy packages and patches, detect unauthorised software, check licence coverage, and find unused licences (s.17.9). LAN/System Administrators should use SMS reporting for regular software-inventory reviews (s.19.5.1). SMS is a **2008 snapshot**, not a live product standard.

Executable files may not be downloaded to a user’s workstation without permission of the Head of CSB (or usually the Intellectual Property Compliance Officer) under IPD’s *Handbook on Management of Intellectual Property in the Government* (s.17.8).

## IPD 3/2005 and OGCIO 3/2008 (Appendix D)

Colleagues should know two circulars, **re-circulated every six months**:

- **Intellectual Property Department General Circular No. 3/2005** — salient provisions of the Copyright Ordinance and what colleagues must note.
- **OGCIO Circular No. 3/2008** — *Intellectual Property Rights Protection through Proper Management and Use of Software*; SAM implementation guidelines.

s.17.8 also points to IPD’s FAQ on copyright at the IPD website.

## SM2 (Appendix D)

**SM2** is the Bureau Intellectual Property Compliance Officer for **software** asset management in CSB. Contact SM2 on procurement and installation of computer software.

Photocopying of copyrighted works is a **different** officer: **SEO(Adm)3**, under the CSB–HKRRLS licence. Do not conflate the two IP Compliance Officers.

Whether SM2 still holds that role after 2008 is a live-system question; do not guess it into this page.
