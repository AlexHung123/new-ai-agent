# Synthesis — what GITP is actually regulating

GITP is not a bulk contract and not a waiver of SPR. It is a **listed-supplier quotation channel** for three product families, forced through one system ([[e-ps]]), with rank tables that get stricter when you do not invite everyone. The wiki’s job is to make that channel readable against SPR, not to replace SPR.

## 1. The listing is optional on both sides

[[dpo]] maintains [[gitp-providers]] who have undertaken the Participation Terms, and publishes a reference IT products list on ITG InfoStation. AP s.1.2(f) is the structural sentence: B/Ds are **not obliged to buy** and the GITP is **not obliged to sell** those products. Providers may offer items that are not on the reference list (AP s.3.2.1). Reference technical and maintenance requirements are “for reference only” (AP s.1.2(g)). Treat the list as a catalogue of who is eligible to be invited in a sub-category, not as a store.

## 2. GITP stops at the quotation gate

AP s.3.3.1 is explicit. At or below the prevailing [[quotation-limit]] in SPR 220(a), invite GITP Providers (normally all of the sub-category). **Over** that limit, arrange an **open tender** under SPR Chapter III. SPR 205 is quoted in full: do not split a requirement or shorten a contract to stay inside the gate; Controlling Officers may be held personally responsible. Urgent buys **not exceeding $5,000** may go as direct local purchase under SPR 265 or 290 and **leave the scheme** (AP s.3.10). GITP does not create a mid-band between quotation and tender.

## 3. Competition is the default; the rank table prices the exception

Two columns run through invitation (AP s.3.3.2) and acceptance (AP s.3.7): *all relevant providers invited* versus *not all invited* (or, on acceptance, not the lowest conforming / highest combined score). Skipping a listed provider needs a reason — the only worked example is debarment after a related system-implementation contract (Note 5). Fewer invitees, or a non-default recommendation, **raises the approving rank**, up to D1 at the quotation ceiling. See [[compare-approving-authorities]].

## 4. The Brief is the contract-facing specification

Almost every later dispute is pre-decided in the [[brief]]: evaluation basis (overall / group / item), marking scheme or price-only, 5-year cost versus one-off, validity, liquidated-damages daily rate, maintenance scheme, trade-in, and any non-default implementation timetable (AP s.3.2). Partial proposals are fatal on an overall basis and fatal within a group on a group basis (AP s.3.5.1). If it is not in the Brief, it is not an evaluation criterion.

## 5. Acceptance tests are the payment gate

Hardware and software must pass installation/function tests and a **four-week** [[acceptance-tests|reliability test]] before an Acceptance Certificate (AP s.4). Payment for products is due only after that certificate; maintenance is quarterly in arrears **after** the [[warranty]] (AP s.5.1). Silence during the reliability window is treated by the Contractor as a pass (AP s.4.3). That is a procedural trap, not a courtesy.

## 6. Integrity rules are SPR’s, restated

RESTRICTED from receipt/opening until a decision (AP s.8.1). Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201) and CSB Circular 4/2007 (AP s.8.2). [[conflict-of-interest]] declarations under SPR 185–186 and CSB Circular 2/2004 for anyone who writes specifications or evaluates (AP s.8.3). Approaching a provider for product information before invitation must not give a perceived advantage (AP s.3.2.1). GITP does not add a new ethics code; it points at the existing one.

## 7. The conversion is a procedure booklet, not the contract and not the annexes

Capitalised terms live in Clause 1 of the [[conditions-of-contract]] (AP s.1.1) — a different instrument. Annexes 1–14 (flowchart, Outline of Brief, maintenance schemes, letters, test reports, CPAR) are **named but not transcribed**. Category A5/A6 table cells are merged in OCR. The filename says v1.3c; the amendment table stops at 1.3 (Feb 2024). For a live buy, use the current DPO booklet, SPR, and e-PS screens — not this wiki.
