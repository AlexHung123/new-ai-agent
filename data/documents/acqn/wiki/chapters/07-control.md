# Control

AP s.8 is the compliance overlay on every GITP buy: a named coordinating officer, classification, bribery and [[conflict-of-interest]] rules, [[value-for-money]], [[trade-in]] / disposal, and data-bearing spare parts. It restates SPR and CSB instruments; it does not replace them.

## Coordinating Officer and classification (AP s.8.1)

Appoint an officer **not lower than Assistant Supplies Officer / Executive Officer II or equivalent** as Coordinating Officer(s) to ensure the booklet’s acquisition procedures are fully complied with.

All correspondence in relation to Proposals, from the time they are **received and opened** to the time a **decision is made** on acceptance or otherwise, must be classified **RESTRICTED**.

## Prevention of bribery (AP s.8.2)

All personnel involved must observe the **Prevention of Bribery Ordinance** (Cap. 201) and **Civil Service Bureau Circular No. 4/2007** on advantages/entertainment offered to an officer in official capacity and gifts and donations to a department for the benefit of staff.

## Conflict of interest (AP s.8.3)

All personnel involved in **preparation of technical specifications** and **proposal evaluation** must declare any conflict of interest in accordance with **SPR 185 and 186**, and observe **CSB Circular No. 2/2004**. Declarations are also required in the invitation-approval and acceptance-approval packs (AP ss.3.3.3, 3.7). See [[conflict-of-interest]].

Approaching a provider for product information before invitation must not give a perceived advantage (AP s.3.2.1).

## Value for money (AP s.8.4)

To achieve the best-value purchases when ordering against GITP:

- **aggregate**, as far as possible, departmental requirements for hardware, software, and services to obtain bulk purchase discount — small-amount purchases only in urgent cases; and
- the Government **does not confer any exclusive right** on [[gitp-providers]] to supply any hardware, software, or services to B/Ds.

See [[value-for-money]] and SPR 205 on [[quotation-limit]].

## Disposal of IT equipment / parts (AP s.8.5)

When acquiring [[trade-in]] for used IT equipment/parts, follow “Destruction of Classified Information” in the IT Security Documents so classified information is securely deleted **before** trade-in. ITG InfoStation: IT Security → IT Security FAQ → Destruction of Classified Information.

For Trade-in Items governed by the **Product Eco-responsibility Ordinance** (Cap. 603), the **Waste Disposal Ordinance** (Cap. 354), and their subsidiary regulations (e.g. regulated electrical equipment; chemical wastes), the Contractor must comply with the statutory requirements (waste disposal licence, export permit, collection/storage/packaging/labelling of e-waste and chemical waste) in executing the Contract and in any subsequent transfer to third parties or export.

Regulated electrical equipment at delivery: [[03-acceptance]] (AP s.4.5). Alternative to trade-in: SPR Chapter XI and GLD Circular 3/2022 (AP s.3.2.5).

## Replacement of parts (AP s.8.6)

Under Clause 1.11 of the Reference Maintenance Service Requirements, parts removed during hardware maintenance **become the property of the Contractor**, provided the Government may **retain** any part to be replaced if the Contractor is unable to erase all data stored in any form in that part. Before removal, the Contractor must certify in writing that all information in the part has been completely erased, and is liable for loss or damage from possession or use of any information remaining.

Under Clause 1.14, if storage media (e.g. a hard disk) itself requires replacement, the Contractor must use an erasure method (e.g. software-based overwriting, degaussing, physical destruction) **approved by B/Ds**, carried out **under the witness of the Government Representative** at a location approved by the Government. The Contractor issues certificate(s) signed with company chop certifying complete erasure, and indemnifies the Government under **Clause 23** of the [[conditions-of-contract]] for any data remaining.
