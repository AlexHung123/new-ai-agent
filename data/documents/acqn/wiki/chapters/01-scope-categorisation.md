# Scope and product categorisation

AP s.2 draws the scheme boundary: four kinds of supply, three major [[product-categories]], and the rule that each IT product sits in **one sub-category only**. Out-of-scope services (custom development; on-site ongoing office/network support) are listed at AP s.3.2.1 and discussed on [[brief]]. Comparison of A / B / C: [[compare-product-categories]].

## Scope (AP s.2.1)

The [[gitp-scheme]] covers supply of IT products and provision of related services to B/Ds as follows (the conversion drops the “i)” marker; treated as four items):

1. Supply of IT products
2. Provision of product-related **support** services
3. Provision of product-related **maintenance** services
4. Provision of product **[[trade-in]]** services

Related-service examples in AP s.1.2(b): one-off support (system configuration, network cabling, network configuration, data erasing, support staff for IT-product tasks), maintenance of offered products, and trade-in. AP s.3.2.1 tightens this: services acquired through GITP should only relate to set-up, configuration, and support of **day-to-day operation** of related IT products under the scheme.

**Not in scope** (AP s.3.2.1 — restated here because it is a scope rule):

- development and/or maintenance of custom program(s)
- on-going support of office systems and/or networks by staff stationed at user site

## Major categories (AP s.2.2)

| Category | Cover |
| --- | --- |
| **A** | Network products and infrastructure equipment (A1–A8) |
| **B** | Server systems (B1–B4) |
| **C** | Microcomputer equipment (C1–C5) |

Each product is classified into **one sub-category only**. A Brief may be issued to one or more sub-categories of one or more major categories (AP s.3.1). Minimum invitation time differs: at least **two weeks** for A and B, **one week** for C (AP s.3.3.4). Full sub-category table: [[product-categories]].
