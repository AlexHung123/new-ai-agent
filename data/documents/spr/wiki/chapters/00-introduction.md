# Introduction — Validity of Government Regulations

SPR 1–16 is the common front matter of the [[government-regulations|Government Regulations]]: what they cover, who may amend or except each volume, the force of circulars, and the duty to know and circulate them. *Stores and Procurement Regulations* is **Volume 4**, made under the Public Finance Ordinance (Cap. 2). Making authority is the [[sfst|Financial Secretary / SFST]]; [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]] may apply, interpret, and except SPR and related Financial Circulars (SPR 4, footnote). SPR 17–99 are blank. The procurement regime itself starts at [[01-authority]].

## What the Regulations cover (SPR 1–3)

Government Regulations govern the conduct of government business, appointment terms and conditions of service, financial and accounting operations, the management and procurement of government stores and services, the security of government records, and miscellaneous matters. They do not spell out necessary and self-evident exceptions (SPR 1).

Save for the Financial and Accounting Regulations and the Stores and Procurement Regulations — both made under Cap. 2 — the Regulations are made by or with the authority of the Chief Executive. The Chief Executive, or officers to whom that authority is delegated, is the sole authority for interpretation and application (SPR 2).

They bind all government servants unless a contrary intention appears; an enactment makes an alternative provision for particular servants; a regulation is inconsistent with an enactment that applies to them; or the terms of employment exclude a regulation (SPR 3).

## Seven volumes (SPR 4)

The named officer may amend, supplement, apply, interpret, and make exceptions to the volume shown, subject to the footnotes.

**PS(Tsy) footnote.** [[pstsy]] is authorised to **apply, interpret, and make exceptions** to SPR and related Financial Circulars — not, on this footnote, to amend the volume itself.

**Volume 7 footnote.** The two Permanent Secretaries may apply, interpret, and except External Service Regulations only where the Regulations say so, and only for staff posted to HKSAR offices outside Hong Kong.

## Circulars have equal force (SPR 5–6)

Each volume may be supplemented by circulars and circular memoranda. Those instructions have **equal application and force** to the Regulations (SPR 5). For SPR the pairing is Financial Circulars and FSTB Circular Memoranda (SFST) plus Government Logistics Department Circulars and Circular Memoranda ([[dgl|DGL]]). Other volumes have their own series (General Circulars; CSB Circulars; Accounting Circulars; Security Circulars; Accommodation Circulars issued by the Government Property Administrator — *source table for Volumes 5–7 is OCR-broken; issuing titles reconstructed from readable cells*).

Each circular series is numbered afresh each calendar year. The first circular of the year lists which earlier circulars remain in force. Circular memoranda may be numbered in the same way and are reviewed regularly (SPR 6).

## Duty to know (SPR 10–11)

Every government servant **other than staff on daily rates of pay** must be thoroughly acquainted with the Regulations, circulars, and circular memoranda on Conduct and Discipline, and with those that appertain to their duties (SPR 10). Disobedience, neglect, or failure to observe the Conduct and Discipline instruments may attract disciplinary proceedings; the officer may also be held pecuniarily responsible for any resulting financial loss to the Government (SPR 11).

## Distribution (SPR 12–14, 16)

Heads of Department must provide, or make available, those instruments to staff other than daily-rated staff (SPR 12). Conduct and Discipline issues exist as a separate edition, including a Chinese version, and must be given or made available on first appointment (SPR 13). Other copies are office equipment, not personal property; they must not be retained on transfer (SPR 14).

Civil Service Bureau, Financial, and General Circulars are issued on one of four scales (SPR 16):

| Scale | Bring to the attention of |
| --- | --- |
| A | All staff |
| B | A particular group, because of conditions of service, salaries, entitlements, or rules that apply to them as individuals |
| C | Officers who, by function of post, must act on or be informed of the circular |
| D | Officers who keep a copy of a particular set of Regulations, and those who by function of post must act on or be informed of the circular |

Circular memoranda have no fixed scale. The normal send-list is Directors of Bureaux, Permanent Secretaries, and Heads of Department, with a copy to the Judiciary Administrator; any of scales A–D may be used if needed.
