# Chapter IV — Consultants selection

[[consultants-selection]] is used only to obtain services from consulting firms, and only when [[tendering]] is a poor fit (SPR 400). Default appointing authority is [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]]. The boards sit under [[ccsb]]. Execution after appointment follows [[05-contract-admin]]. The pre-contract negotiation article is unnumbered in the OCR compilation and is treated as SPR 455 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR).

## When to use, and exceptions (SPR 400–405)

Consultants selection should be used only where, for example, Government lacks the necessary expertise; quality of the end product depends on the consultant’s experience, capability, reputation, and method; the qualities required cannot be precisely specified or quantified; or quality is of paramount importance and selection by competitive tendering on price is likely to lower quality (SPR 400).

Separate procedures apply, and these Regulations do not, for consultancy not exceeding the [[quotation]] limit in SPR 220(a); employment of individual persons; and briefing-out of legal work by the Secretary for Justice (SPR 405).

## Approving authority and funding (SPR 410, 416)

Unless authority is specifically delegated to departments and consultants selection boards, PS(Tsy) is the approving authority for appointing consulting firms. Before proposing an appointment, the procuring department must obtain policy support from the relevant Director of Bureau (or an officer authorised by the Director), and must confirm that qualified staff are not available in the department, elsewhere in Government, or within the required timeframe. Financial consultancies, and non-financial consultancies that engage financial consultants as sub-consultants, need the endorsement of the MA Division of FSTB. General management consultancies need the support of DPO (SPR 410).

For works-related consultancies funded under the Capital Works Reserve Fund, [[controlling-officer|COs]] shall adopt [[parallel-tendering]]; SPR 337(a) and 338 apply equally. For other consultancies, COs may adopt it; SPR 337(b) and 338 apply equally (SPR 416).

## Classification (SPR 420)

Communications on selection, from the time a shortlist or a list for direct invitation is proposed until an agreement is executed, must be classified RESTRICTED (CONTRACT). Consultancy agreements are not usually classified; RESTRICTED (CONTRACT) is normally adequate for sensitive disputes, litigation, or claims. CONFIDENTIAL is used only if disclosure would prejudice the interest of the HKSAR Government.

## Boards and committees (SPR 425–427)

[[sfst|The Financial Secretary / SFST]] appoints consultants selection boards of not less than three persons, each chaired by a directorate officer not lower than D2, and has appointed —

- **CCSB**, which advises PS(Tsy) on selection and appointment of all consultants other than those handled by AACSB, EACSB, and DCSCs. SPR 430–470 apply.
- **AACSB**, which approves architectural and associated consultants for government projects other than those appointed by DCSCs (Architectural Services Department handbook and DEVB TC(W)).
- **EACSB**, which approves engineering and associated consultants other than those appointed by DCSCs (EACSB handbook and DEVB TC(W)).

SFST has delegated to COs the authority to approve selection and appointment up to the departmental limit in SPR 220(a) ($10 million). That delegation covers the list of firms, consultancy briefs, marking schemes, pre-contract negotiations, appointment, and cancellation. COs shall appoint a **DCSC** of not less than three persons for those assignments. The DCSC chair must be a directorate officer not lower than D2 and at least one rank higher than the chair of the assessment panel. Roles of the DCSC, the assessment panel, and other departmental officers must be segregated.

All marking schemes for consultancy proposals must be approved by the relevant board or DCSC. Technical weighting should normally be around 60–70%, unless justified to that board or DCSC (SPR 427).

## CCSB two-stage process (SPR 430–445)

CCSB normally uses a two-stage approval (SPR 430(a)). Stage 1 approves inviting proposals from the recommended firms, the consultancy brief, and the method of assessment. Stage 2 approves appointment of the recommended firm, subject to fees and other terms in the negotiating brief being resolved.

Authority to approve a **direct approach to one firm** without competition rests with PS(Tsy) only. It is **not** delegated to COs at any value (SPR 430(b)). Approval is not normally granted without full justification: why competition is not used, scope, estimated cost, and the required expertise. After PS(Tsy) approval, the department approaches the firm, assesses the single proposal, and sends a combined Stage 1 and 2 submission to CCSB (above the SPR 220(a) departmental limit) or the DCSC (at or below that limit).

Submissions to CCSB are six copies including the original, at least seven clear working days before the meeting, signed or endorsed by the CO or a directorate representative.

Departments may keep lists of consulting firms where there is a frequent need (SPR 435). MA Division keeps a list of accounting, financial, and actuary firms; DPO keeps a list of management consulting firms. Lists and the method of application must be published on the department’s webpage for as long as they remain valid. New applications must be allowed at any time and processed promptly.

**Stage 1 (SPR 440).** The department compiles a long list (from SPR 435 lists, or by advertisement) and decides whether to invite expressions of interest or to invite proposals direct. EOI is useful if expertise is widely available, or if market interest is unclear; direct invitation is more time- and cost-effective if expertise is highly specialised or the consultant must be on board quickly. An assessment panel, normally government officials only and chaired by a directorate officer of the department, endorses the list or the short-listing criteria. If EOI is used, firms should normally have no less than two weeks and should not be asked for full proposals at that stage. All firms meeting the criteria should be short-listed. The draft brief is cleared with [[doj|D of J]] and, if necessary, DPO and MA Division. The marking scheme, including the 60–70% technical weighting unless CCSB is satisfied otherwise, is designed at this stage. A specimen Stage 1 submission is at Appendix IV(A).

**Stage 2 (SPR 445).** After Stage 1 approval, the approved brief is issued. Firms should generally have no less than three weeks. Technical and fee proposals go in separate sealed envelopes marked “Technical Proposal” or “Fee Proposal”. Fees are on a fixed lump sum or a time-charge basis; time-charge proposals must be capped so that cost can be compared (SPR 465). The estimated contract value should not be disclosed unless it is already public. The assessment panel completes the technical assessment first under the approved scheme; the scheme must not be altered without CCSB’s agreement. Fee envelopes are opened only after technical assessment is finished. The firm with the highest overall score should normally be recommended. The Stage 2 submission should include a fallback if agreement cannot be reached. A specimen is at Appendix IV(B).

## Negotiations, acceptance, pay, cancellation (SPR 455–470)

Unless authority is specifically delegated, PS(Tsy) approves pre-contract negotiations under the CCSB procedures. The authorised negotiator should be at directorate level unless PS(Tsy) approves otherwise. Only officers with no actual, potential, or perceived [[conflict-of-interest]] may lead or participate; they must declare under SPR 186. Consultancy procurement is **not bound by [[wto-gpa]]**. Officers must still observe fair play and transparency, and may use Appendix III(J) (SPR 455(e)).

If the negotiating brief is resolved, the department tells the Secretary of CCSB, draws up an agreement cleared with D of J, and invites the firm to sign. Unless PS(Tsy) authorises otherwise, the officer signing for Government should be at directorate level. SPR 505–510 then apply (SPR 460).

Remuneration may be time charges, a lump sum by instalments, or a combination (SPR 465). Time charge suits assignments whose duration and extent cannot be fixed in advance (investigations, economic appraisals, research). Rates cover professional, technical, and supporting staff plus overheads and profit; identifiable special equipment and other expenditure are reimbursed. Detailed time records, inspectable payroll records, and receipts are required. A **ceiling** is normally placed on the total. Lump sum is used when scope and duration can be defined clearly. Sums should generally be in Hong Kong dollars; SPR 355(b) applies if firms are asked to quote in a foreign currency other than US dollars for contracts likely to cost HK$10 million or more. The total fee may be disclosed to a third party such as the Legislative Council or the media; individual staff rates must not.

If a department decides to cancel for public interest, lack of conforming bids, or a service level agreement with a trading-fund department, it must obtain prior approval of the relevant board, with detailed justification, and then promptly inform bidders (SPR 470). For assignments not exceeding the SPR 220(a) departmental limit, that authority is delegated to DCSCs. Approval is not required when no proposals are received, or, in parallel tendering, if [[finance-committee|Finance Committee]] does not approve the funding application.
