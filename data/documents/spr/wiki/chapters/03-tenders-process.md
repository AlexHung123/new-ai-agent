## Notices and periods (SPR 340)

Gazette notices follow General Regulations 103–105. Three bilingual draft copies go to the Official Languages Division of CSB by 3:30 p.m. on Tuesday; a further copy goes to [[dgl|DGL]] by 2:30 p.m. Notices generally appear in two consecutive Gazette issues. Departments may also advertise on the Internet, in the press, or in journals.

For [[wto-gpa|WTO GPA]] procurements, tender notices and prequalification invitations shall be published in the Gazette. For works contracts not exceeding $52 million and other procurements not subject to WTO GPA, Internet-only publication suffices (FC No. 3/2009); Gazette or press publication is optional.

Invitations must state the office that issues documents, the tender-box location, and the closing date and time. Late or misplaced tenders will not be accepted. Notices must say whether the procurement is covered by WTO GPA, and must warn of possible extension if tropical cyclone signal No. 8 or above, a black rainstorm warning, or “extreme conditions after super typhoons” is in force, or if public access to the box is blocked.

A minimum of three weeks is normally required. For non-GPA works not exceeding $52 million and other non-GPA procurements, [[controlling-officer|COs]] may allow a shorter period commensurate with complexity, normally not less than ten days (FC No. 3/2009). For GPA, at least 40 days for receipt of tenders and no less than 25 days for prequalification applications. Extreme urgency to shorten a GPA period needs prior approval of [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]]; that authority is delegated to DGL (or designated officers) for GLD tenders and for stores not exceeding the SPR 220(a) departmental limit.

## Documents (SPR 345)

Departments should normally use Tender for the Supply of Goods (GF 230), Tender for Services (GF 231), Tender for the Purchase of Articles or Materials from the Government (GF 232), the works Articles of Agreement and General Conditions, GLD Standard Terms and Conditions, or DPO Standard Terms and Conditions for IT tenders. Special Conditions may delete or amend standard clauses after consulting [[doj|D of J]] or, for works, LAD(W)/DEVB. Non-standard forms must be cleared with D of J or LAD(W)/DEVB before use.

The standard forms at SPR 345(a)(iv)–(vi) must include clauses allowing the Government to disqualify a tenderer who has engaged, is engaging, or is reasonably believed to have engaged or be engaging in acts likely to constitute or cause offences endangering [[national-security]], or otherwise if it is in the interest of national security, and to terminate the contract in that interest. Departments must incorporate such clauses. If they consider the clauses unsuitable, they must seek prior clearance from the relevant policy bureau and D of J and/or LAD(W)/DEVB.

A complete set covers Terms of Tender, General and Special Conditions, Offer to be Bound or Form of Tender, specifications (SPR 350), and bills of quantities or price schedules where applicable. For a non-works contract estimated to exceed $300 million, all non-standard components (bills of quantities, particular specifications, special conditions) must go to D of J before issue. If those vetted documents later need substantial amendment, they must be vetted again before an addendum or award. At or below $300 million, the CO (or a designated officer not below Chief Supplies Officer / Chief Executive Officer or equivalent) decides whether legal vetting is required.

## Access to information (SPR 346–349)

Disclosure must not undermine the integrity of the tendering system and must observe the Code on Access to Information. Departments should not disclose the estimated contract value unless it has already been made public; if it has, all known potential tenderers must be told. Essential clarifications go to all known potential tenderers (or on the department’s website for open tender) before closing. An unsuccessful tenderer is entitled to know why, without revealing another tenderer’s confidential information.

The public may be given particulars of a contract, the category of tenderers invited, the closing date and time, the number of tenders received, the name of the successful tenderer, the accepted sum, and the award date — the last items only after the contract is executed. Under no circumstances should a department release minutes of the tender board or committee, or another tenderer’s method statement and plan, financial position, technical capability, or information given in confidence, without that tenderer’s express agreement.

## Specifications and marking schemes (SPR 350)

Specifications must follow SPR 106–109A and must not create unnecessary obstacles to trade or competition. They should be functional and performance-based, not built around a particular model. Trademarks, patents, origin, or named producers are allowed only if there is no other precise description, and then only with words such as “or equivalent”. Over-prescription and input-based requirements risk incumbent advantage and work against [[value-for-money]].

As a general rule, tenderer’s experience should not be an essential requirement in non-works tenders, irrespective of value. If experience must be essential, it must not be imposed to avoid competition, and must be non-discriminatory, relevant, and current; justifications go in the tender report. Experience may instead be a desirable criterion in a [[marking-scheme]].

For non-works tenders, marking schemes should follow the SMS Framework in FC No. 2/2019. The normally allowed range is technical 50–70% and price 30–50%. A minimum percentage of technical marks must be reserved for innovative suggestions. Schemes within the Framework need no prior board or [[dtc|DTC]] approval. A CO may designate directorate officers to approve a technical weighting below 50% on an exceptional basis (simple, straightforward goods or services with little likely technical variation). A technical weighting above 70%, or any other departure from the Framework, needs prior board or DTC approval. For service contracts that rely heavily on non-skilled workers, no exception is allowed for a technical weighting below 50%, or for assigning fewer than 25 marks out of 100 on “wages”.

## Currency, deposits, retention (SPR 355–363)

Contract sums should generally be quoted and paid in Hong Kong dollars. Foreign currency may be allowed if goods manufactured outside Hong Kong form a significant part of the contract or the estimated “overseas” element exceeds HK$500,000, in the currency of the supplying country; local materials and labour stay in Hong Kong dollars. Tenders likely to cost HK$10 million or more cannot be quoted in a foreign currency other than US dollars unless PS(Tsy) is satisfied that a refusal would significantly reduce competition. For comparison, foreign quotations convert at the Hong Kong Association of Banks selling rate on the tender closing date.

A tender deposit or bond is not normally required (SPR 360). Successful tenderers may be required to pay a contract deposit or provide a performance bond before signing (SPR 361). For works, follow the relevant DEVB TC(W). For non-works contracts, unless the CO personally approves otherwise, SPR 362 requires —

| Contract value | Deposit or performance bond (% of contract value) |
| --- | --- |
| Stores or services ≤ the SPR 220(a) quotation limit | Not required |
| Above that quotation limit and ≤ $15 million | 2% |
| Above $15 million | 2% if financial vetting is passed; otherwise 5% for low-risk contracts or 6% for high-risk contracts. High-risk, long-duration contracts also need periodic vetting and closer monitoring. |
| Revenue contracts | Not required, but departments may vet and/or require a deposit in individual cases. |

Deposits are not refunded until the contract is completed. Retention money is normally pitched at no more than 5% of the contract value (SPR 363).

## Opening, evaluation, reports (SPR 365–375)

The block after SPR 363 on opening and clarification is unnumbered; treated as SPR 365 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR). Opening starts when the deadline falls. Missing information that is an “essential requirement” makes the tender non-conforming; departments must not then approach the tenderer. Factual information that cannot be manipulated by late submission may be sought. A department may not reject a tender as a qualified bid without the relevant board’s approval, except that for non-GPA works not exceeding $52 million, and for procurements not exceeding the SPR 220(a) departmental limit, that authority is transferred to COs and DTCs respectively.

Evaluation is normally by a TAP of not less than two persons (SPR 370). The TAP tests conformity with specifications, essential requirements, and conditions. Due diligence follows GLD operational guidelines (non-works) or DEVB guidelines (works). Factors include technical and financial capability and past performance; timely delivery; compatibility; after-sale support; running and maintenance costs; fair market prices; and the interest of national security. For non-works stores or service contracts exceeding $15 million, financial vetting of the tenderer under consideration is required unless the CO personally approves otherwise (Appendix III(H)). Where a marking scheme is used, the tender that attains any passing marks and the highest overall score should normally be recommended. If tendered sums are close or payments span years, present value should prevail; MA Division of FSTB may advise. If none of the tenders fully conforms or attains passing marks, cancel and retender (SPR 380(e)). Recommending a non-conforming tender is exceptional and should be cleared with D of J or LAD(W)/DEVB.

Tender reports follow Appendix III(I) (SPR 375). Reports to [[ctb|CTB]] and subsidiary boards must be signed or endorsed by the Head of Department or a directorate representative. DTC reports are signed by the TAP chairman. If ranking is by price and the recommended tender is the lowest (highest for revenue), only the three lowest (highest) tenders go to the board; otherwise the lower (higher) tenders, the recommended tender, and the next two. If ranking is by overall score, the same pattern uses the three highest scorers. For works that COs approve personally under SPR 310(h), SPR 375(a)–(c) do not apply.

## Cancellation, negotiation, award (SPR 380–390)

Unless it is not in the public interest to award, the department shall award to the capable tenderer that submitted the most advantageous tender on the stated criteria, or the lowest (highest for revenue) where price is the sole criterion (SPR 380). If a trading-fund department submits the lowest or most advantageous tender, the procuring department shall enter into a service level agreement and promptly tell all tenderers that no “contract” will be issued in the “public interest”. Cancellation needs no approval if no tenders are received, or, in [[parallel-tendering]], if [[finance-committee|Finance Committee]] does not approve funding before expiry of validity. Otherwise, prior approval of the relevant board or DTC is required. For non-GPA works not exceeding $30 million, COs may personally approve cancellation.

The compilation numbers SPR 385 as “5.” (source unclear; verify against printed SPR). Notices must reserve the right to negotiate. PS(Tsy) is the approving authority and has delegated it to subsidiary boards within their purview; DGL or designated officers for GLD tenders; COs or designated directorate officers who were not involved in the exercise, for non-GPA works not exceeding $52 million; and DTCs within the SPR 220(a) departmental limit. PS(Tsy) has also delegated to COs (or such uninvolved directorate officers) authority to approve negotiations for their own department’s tenders when a single tender under SPR 325 has been invited; when only one or very few open tenders have been received and may not be competitive; when the recommended price is too high (or too low for revenue) against past or market information; when the recommended tender contains disadvantageous counter-proposals that do not render it non-conforming; or when two or more conforming tenders are equal and equally most advantageous. Negotiators must have no real or potential [[conflict-of-interest]].

Unless authority is specifically delegated, PS(Tsy) is the approving authority for acceptance (SPR 390). Subsidiary boards and DTCs accept within their limits. After a decision, the board secretary notifies the department (copy to the Director of Audit). The department accepts by letter or invites the successful tenderer to sign, and promptly tells unsuccessful tenderers why they failed. If the accepted sum exceeds approved estimates, the CO must obtain an increase in commitment or project estimate first. Departments shall publish a notice of award on their Internet home page unless, after legal advice and policy-bureau support, PS(Tsy) approves non-publication because it would be contrary to national security or public interest. They must keep a record of all awards, and a separate record of each single or restricted tender and each direct engagement. Award and execution then follow SPR 505–510.
