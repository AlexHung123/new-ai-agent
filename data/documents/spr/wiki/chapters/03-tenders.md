# Chapter III — Tender procedures (scope to funding)

Chapter III of the Stores and Procurement Regulations sets the default [[tendering]] route for stores, services, works, other items, and revenue contracts. Open competition is the norm; the alternatives are compared at [[compare-tendering-types]]. [[wto-gpa|WTO GPA]] overlays extra rules where the estimated maximum total value over the whole duration (all remuneration, one or more contractors) meets the GPA threshold. Quotation-level buying is in [[02-procurement]]. Process from notice to award is in [[03-tenders-process]].

## Scope and exceptions (SPR 300)

The compilation heading “Scope and coverage” is unnumbered; treated as SPR 300 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR). Tender procedures apply except for —

- stores, services, and revenue contracts not exceeding the [[quotation]] limits in SPR 220, which follow quotation procedures;
- franchises, concessions, leases, licences, tenancies, and other items procured or disposed of by public auction or a method laid down by statute, Government Regulations, or an administrative procedure agreed by [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]];
- private treaty grants, exchanges, extensions, and short-term tenancies of land under the approved Abbreviated Tender System;
- consultancy services through quotation and the consultants selection procedures in Chapters II and IV (see [[04-consultants]]);
- [[direct-engagement]] of contractors, suppliers, or service providers under SPR 331–333;
- briefing-out of legal work by the Secretary for Justice; and
- employment of individual persons.

Where a procurement is covered by [[wto-gpa]], departments must also comply with GPA and the related circulars. Value for that test is the estimated maximum total value over the entire duration.

## Classification of information (SPR 305)

All communications regarding tenders, from the time tender documents are prepared until a decision on acceptance, must be classified RESTRICTED (TENDER). Correspondence on prequalification and single or restricted tendering is also RESTRICTED (TENDER). Contract documents are not usually classified; RESTRICTED is adequate for sensitive disputes, litigation, or claims. CONFIDENTIAL is used only if disclosure would prejudice the interest of the HKSAR Government. [[controlling-officer|Controlling Officers]] and chairmen of tender boards appoint officers to open and handle classified correspondence on a need-to-know basis, under the Security Regulations.

## Tender boards and approving authorities (SPR 310)

The compilation heading is unnumbered; treated as SPR 310 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR).

[[sfst|The Financial Secretary / SFST]] appoints the [[ctb|Central Tender Board]]. PS(Tsy) is authorised to appoint subsidiary tender boards. COs appoint [[dtc|Departmental Tender Committees]] of not less than three persons to consider or decide on acceptance, or to advise on acceptance, up to the financial limits. The relationship among CTB, subsidiary boards, and DTCs is in SPR 116.

CTB advises PS(Tsy) on acceptance of all tenders exceeding the financial limits of subsidiary boards, and on tenders, contracts, and subsidiary boards generally. In case of serious disagreement among CTB members on an award, the Chairman may refer the matter to FS/SFST. Submissions to CTB (seven copies plus the original) must be signed or endorsed by the CO or a directorate representative.

There are currently two subsidiary tender boards: the GLD Tender Board and the Public Works Tender Board. Division of opinion on a subsidiary board is referred to CTB. Each subsidiary board must, at month-end, report to CTB every award that did not go to the lowest conforming offer (highest for a revenue contract) or, where a marking scheme is used, the highest overall scorer, with reasons.

COs must establish a DTC of not less than three persons, chaired by a directorate officer not lower than D2 who is at least one rank higher than the chairman of the Tender Assessment Panel (TAP) for that exercise. Division of opinion on a DTC may be referred to the CO. DTCs make the same month-end reports to the relevant tender board.

COs are delegated authority to personally approve —

- the award of all works contracts not exceeding $30 million each and not subject to WTO GPA; and
- the award of works contracts above $30 million but not exceeding $52 million each and not subject to WTO GPA, provided they are awarded to the lowest conforming bidder or the highest overall scorer.

## Types of tendering (SPR 315–330)

Departments should normally adopt open tendering. Alternatives require approval (SPR 315). See [[compare-tendering-types]].

**Open tendering (SPR 316).** All interested contractors, suppliers, or service providers may submit tenders. Publication of notices is in SPR 340.

**Selective tendering (SPR 320).** The compilation heading is unnumbered; treated as SPR 320 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR). Invitation is confined to the relevant approved list. It is a two-step open bidding process used where there is a frequent need and not all market participants are capable. Admission to a list is normally accepted at any time. A firm that has asked to join but is not yet listed must not be excluded merely because there is insufficient time to examine the application, except in exceptional cases of complexity.

Except for works, [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]] approves establishment of lists, and any revision of qualification criteria or assessment method. Criteria must not discriminate among foreign firms or between domestic and foreign firms. For GPA, criteria are limited to those essential to legal and financial capacity and commercial and technical ability. A previous contract with a GPA-covered entity must not be required, though relevant prior experience may be required where essential. For works, authority over the List of Approved Contractors for Public Works and the List of Approved Suppliers of Materials and Specialist Contractors for Public Works is delegated to the Permanent Secretary for Development (Works). Updating a list against already approved criteria does not need further PS(Tsy) approval.

**Single and restricted tendering (SPR 325).** The compilation heading is unnumbered; treated as SPR 325 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR). Tenders are invited from only one or a limited number of firms (including limited tendering under WTO GPA). The mode must not be used to avoid competition, to discriminate against non-local firms, or to protect local firms. Examples that may justify it include extreme urgency from unforeseeable events; a work of art, patents, copyrights, other exclusive rights, or absence of competition for technical reasons with no reasonable alternative; a failed open or selective exercise where requirements have not substantially changed; additional deliveries by the original contractor where a change is not economic or technical; a prototype or first good or service developed for research or original development; services by utility companies; lease terms requiring a particular firm; and where, in the interest of [[national-security]], open or selective tendering could not be used.

Prior approval to initiate —

- the CO personally, for stores, services (excluding works and consultancy) and revenue tenders invited by the department within the departmental limit in SPR 220(a) (the same if the department chooses this mode within the quotation limit);
- [[dgl|DGL]] or delegated officers, for GLD tenders; and
- PS(Tsy) for all other tenders.

Requests for single or restricted tenders for stores exceeding the SPR 220(a) departmental limit, other than through a GLD contract, go via DGL with recommendations to PS(Tsy). COs should follow FC No. 2/2019. Requests, irrespective of value, must be signed or endorsed at directorate level. After initiation is approved, recommendations go to the relevant tender board or DTC under SPR 116. Invited tenderers submit in the same manner as open or selective exercises and must not be told that the invitation is single or restricted. Notices go by letter.

**Prequalified tendering (SPR 330).** The compilation heading is unnumbered; treated as SPR 330 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR). Used where a list of financially and technically capable tenderers is needed — for example pre-testing of equipment; extremely complex, high-value, or rigidly programmed projects; high coordination, technical expertise, or a non-standard form (Build-Operate-Transfer or certain Design-and-Build); or products critical to users. PS(Tsy) approves use and evaluation criteria on the advice of CTB. Invitations are published in the Gazette. After assessment against CTB-endorsed criteria, the department recommends a list to CTB. Material changes between prequalification and tender need CTB endorsement. Unless PS(Tsy), on CTB advice, approves otherwise, a prequalification is for a particular project or product and the list normally remains valid for one year.

## Direct engagement (SPR 331–333)

[[direct-engagement]] without tendering should be used only when open, selective, restricted, or single tendering would not be effective, and only with very strong justifications — for example national security, extreme urgency, or protection of patents, copyrights, or other exclusive rights. If the procurement is covered by WTO GPA, departments should refer to Article XIII and, where that article does not apply, Article III, and seek legal advice from [[doj|D of J]] and/or LAD(W)/DEVB if in doubt.

Authority to approve initiation and acceptance rests with PS(Tsy). For stores, services (excluding works and consultancy) and revenue contracts not exceeding the SPR 220(a) departmental limit, that authority is delegated to COs personally. Requests, irrespective of value, must be signed or endorsed at directorate level. Approving officers should follow FC No. 2/2019. In addition to the SPR 325(d) particulars, the submission must cover contracting strategy (protection of the Government’s interest and public perception) and negotiation strategy with a baseline on cost or revenue, if applicable.

## Funding and parallel tendering (SPR 337–338)

The compilation heading “Funding” is unnumbered before SPR 338; treated as SPR 337 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR).

For works contracts funded under the Capital Works Reserve Fund, COs shall adopt [[parallel-tendering]] (invite tenders before funding is secured) unless, after a risk assessment, they consider that the benefits (time saving, greater certainty in the Approved Project Estimate) are outweighed by the risks (abortive work, appearing to pre-empt [[finance-committee|Finance Committee]] / Legislative Council). For estimated value exceeding $30 million, exemption from parallel tendering needs prior approval of the relevant Director of Bureau (or a Permanent Secretary authorised in writing).

For other contracts, COs may adopt parallel tendering if a risk assessment shows that the benefits outweigh the risks. If the estimated value exceeds $30 million, prior approval of the Director of Bureau (or authorised Permanent Secretary) is required to adopt it.

When parallel tendering is used, invitations must state that funding has not been obtained and that the Government is not responsible for bid-preparation costs. COs should not award a contract, or indicate that Government would accept a bid, unless and until funding is secured. Conditions for award are in FC No. 3/2020.
