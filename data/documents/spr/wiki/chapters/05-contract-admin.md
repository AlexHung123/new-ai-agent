# Chapter V — Contract administration

Unless otherwise specified, Chapter V applies to stores, services, and revenue contracts whether they were awarded through [[quotation]], [[tendering]], or [[consultants-selection]] (SPR 500). [[controlling-officer|Controlling Officers]] own administration after award. [[contract-variation|Variations]] have their own authority table in Appendix V(B). The headings for authorised signatories and insurance are unnumbered in the OCR compilation; they are treated as SPR 505 and SPR 510 (source unclear; verify against printed SPR). Later text cites SPR 505(f), which supports that numbering.

## Execution (SPR 505–510)

For tender or consultants selection awards, departments conclude by a letter of acceptance or by signing a formal contract. Before that letter or signature, the department should obtain a certified true copy of the business registration certificate from the Commissioner of Inland Revenue, or evidence of exemption under the Business Registration Ordinance. A limited company should produce a certified true copy of its memorandum and articles and a board resolution authorising the signatory. A partnership should confirm in writing the partner’s authority. For non-Hong Kong counterparties, consult [[doj|D of J]]. If the firm is already on a registered or approved list, annual verification at certificate renewal is enough. Apparent non-compliance with the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) or the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) must be reported to the Registrar of Companies or the Commissioner of Inland Revenue. Guidance on execution is at Appendix V(A).

Save for SPR 505(f), contracts shall be concluded by directorate officers signing on behalf of the Government, unless [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]] has authorised otherwise. If the substance of a document is to conclude a contract, immediately or subject to conditions, a directorate officer must sign it. For Chapter II quotation procedures, COs may designate officers not lower than Assistant Supplies Officer / Executive Officer II or equivalent.

Where the contract requires insurance, the department must see a valid policy and premium receipts before execution, and the officer in charge keeps the policy. If an accident causes personal injury, the Employees’ Compensation Ordinance (Cap. 282) s.15 obliges the firm as employer to notify the Commissioner for Labour. The officer in charge should instruct the firm to copy that notice to the department. Any claim made directly against the Government under that Ordinance, at common law, or under the Fatal Accidents Ordinance (Cap. 22) must be notified immediately to the firm, the Commissioner for Labour, and D of J.

## Payments (SPR 515)

Payments properly due must follow the contract and should normally be made within 30 calendar days upon acceptance of the deliverables. If a payment must be made before all authorities are obtained, prior approval of the Treasury Branch of FSTB is required to open an advance account — only in exceptional circumstances (for example an unexpectedly high arbitration award). The account is on the CO’s personal responsibility and must be cleared as soon as the authorities are obtained.

Foreign-currency contracts are normally paid in the quoted currency. If both parties agree to pay in Hong Kong dollars instead, the date and conversion rate for each payment must be specified in the contract. For a total non-US-dollar commitment below the equivalent of HK$10 million, the department reports known or anticipated foreign-currency requirements to the Hong Kong Monetary Authority at each month-end. For a total non-US-dollar commitment of HK$10 million or more, the department must ask the Treasury to arrange **forward purchase** through HKMA **before** the contract is awarded. The transaction normally takes place on the award day. The requisition must quote the award authority and a schedule of payment dates and amounts.

## Variations and negotiations (SPR 520–525)

Under no circumstances may a department vary a contract so that the approved commitment or approved project estimate is exceeded (SPR 520(a)).

A CO, or a designated officer of appropriate rank, may approve any number of variations (including extension of a contract awarded by the department, and minor variations to [[dgl|GLD]] contracts) that are inevitable and do **not** increase the original contract value — or do not affect it, for a revenue contract — provided all relevant factors, including rates, remain no less favourable. Contingencies (such as a notional value for additional architectural or engineering consultancy services) and optional items contemplated in the contract are **not** counted as part of the original value.

The cumulative value of variations should not normally exceed 50% of the original contract value (again excluding those contingencies and options). Excesses need fully documented justifications and should be a stop-gap unless justified under the current GLD Circular. Any variation that increases original value (or affects a revenue contract’s value), or any increase in contract sums, must be approved by the authority in Appendix V(B). Officers must not evade those limits by packing in items that reduce the apparent increase. Where one variation decreases value and another increases it, only the increase counts toward the accumulated value for Appendix V(B). Where original items are inevitably replaced by new items as part of one variation, only the net value counts.

Variations, especially those not provided for in the original contract, may amount to new procurements. If a variation amounting to a new procurement is covered by [[wto-gpa]], all relevant GPA requirements must be met. In case of doubt, seek D of J or, for works, LAD(W)/DEVB. Approved variations and any supplementary agreements go to the Director of Audit.

If a department proposes to vary terms, it must obtain the SPR 520 / Appendix V(B) authority’s agreement on the variation and on the negotiation strategy or bottom line first (SPR 525). For GLD contracts, PS(Tsy) has delegated approval of contract negotiation to DGL. Negotiations follow the principles in SPR 385 and Appendix III(J).

## Documents (SPR 530)

Departments retain the original executed contract and any original security bond, bank guarantee, and insurance policy, stored in a safe or strongroom if available, otherwise in a locked cabinet whose key is held by the officer responsible. A certified true copy of the executed contract goes to the Director of Audit. Originals must not be kept in working files.

Documents submitted by unsuccessful firms in a tender or consultants selection exercise may be destroyed three months after the relevant contract is executed. For GPA procurements, departments shall keep the tender documents, all documents submitted by tenderers, other documents and reports of tendering and award (including the SPR 390(c) records), and data that ensure electronic traceability, for **not less than three years** from the date of contract award.

## Performance (SPR 535–541)

Departments shall devise an effective monitoring mechanism. For contracts above the SPR 220 quotation limits they must also follow SPR 536–541: set KPIs that reflect the required standard of service; assign staff with appropriate expertise and supervisory ranks; and require supervisors to confirm in writing that monitoring actions are completed. Non-works contracts follow GLD operational guidelines; works follow DEVB.

They shall evaluate performance at least once every six months until completion if the contract lasts more than one year, and upon completion if it lasts a year or less (SPR 536). As soon as performance is unsatisfactory or deteriorating, the department must notify the contractor or consultant in writing, invite an explanation, request improvement, and step up monitoring (SPR 537). If there is still no improvement, consider further contractual action: a written warning that continued failure may lead to termination and/or suspension from bidding for new Government contracts, and suspension of payment if pre-agreed milestones are not met (SPR 538). Departments must take visible and prompt action against non-compliance, and contracts must give Government the right to act where the contractor or consultant departs from specifications or acts outside the contract without prior consent (SPR 538A).

COs oversee administration of procurement contracts under their purview. Departments make annual reports to their COs on monitoring and follow-up under SPR 535–538A. Performance records must be kept for as long as the department considers necessary, and in any case not less than three years after completion (SPR 539). Works contractors follow the Contractor Management Handbook and DEVB TC(W). Consultants appointed by CCSB, AACSB, or EACSB follow Appendix V(C) and the relevant handbooks; DCSC-appointed consultants may follow departmental procedures or the parent board’s arrangements (SPR 540).

Departments shall take into account relevant past performance records, where available, when evaluating a later tender or consultancy proposal (SPR 541).
