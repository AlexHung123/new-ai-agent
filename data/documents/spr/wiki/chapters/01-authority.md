## Making authority and applicability (SPR 100, 105)

SPR is made by the [[sfst|Financial Secretary / SFST]] under section 11(1) of the Public Finance Ordinance (Cap. 2), and is to be read with that Ordinance (SPR 100). It binds all public officers except so far as inconsistent with any enactment. Financial Circulars and FSTB Circular Memoranda have **equal application and force** (SPR 105). GLD Circulars, with [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]] approval, have the same force (SPR 120(c)).

## Policy and four principles (SPR 106–109)

The policy is to obtain stores and services at the best [[value-for-money]] in a publicly accountable manner (SPR 106). Value for money **is not buying at the lowest price**. It is economy, effectiveness, and efficiency on a whole-life costing basis — the optimal use of resources for the best outcome, including social, environmental, economic, and other public value. Controlling Officers must be satisfied that a procurement is genuinely required and not more costly than necessary (SPR 107). Decisions are reviewable; [[wto-gpa]] procurements are also reviewable by the [[review-body|Review Body on Bid Challenges]]. COs remain accountable and must document the justification (SPR 108).

Four principles underpin the policy (SPR 109):

- **Open and fair competition.** Open bidding is the norm. [[direct-engagement|Single/restricted tendering or direct engagement]] is the exception and must be justified to the approving authority. Treat bidders equally; do not discriminate by country of origin; write specifications and [[marking-scheme|marking schemes]] objectively. Where past exercises had thin competition, explore how to attract new bidders.
- **Transparency.** Give potential bidders the same information. Publish open-tender notices and invitations to join supplier lists on departmental webpages. Keep documents clear and short.
- **Pro-innovation.** Prefer outcome-based requirements; avoid over-specification. For stores, non-works services, and revenue contracts, “innovation” covers both technology and non-technology ideas that create public value (cleaner delivery, policy objectives such as employing persons with disability, user-centric design).
- **Integrity.** Impartial decisions; public funds spent accountably.

## National security (SPR 109A)

Safeguarding [[national-security|national security]] is a constitutional duty. COs must assess potential national-security risks **at every stage**, including sub-contracting approvals and on-going contractor monitoring, and take all measures necessary to avoid adverse consequences for the Government (SPR 109A(a)). The duty is **continuing** — pre-award and after award. Standard forms under SPR 345(a) contain disqualification and termination clauses in the interest of national security; departments shall incorporate them (SPR 109A(b)).

Raise specific concerns with the policy bureau at the earliest opportunity. The bureau may consult the Security Bureau; consult [[doj|D of J]] and/or LAD(W)/DEVB on legal issues and [[dgl|GLD]] on procedure (SPR 109A(c)). Note 1 applies **regardless of value, means, or type**: review publicly available and other information on tenderers’ background and track record (offences or activities endangering national security; PRC sanctions; affiliations that may pose a national-security risk) and the nature of the buy (for example, support to critical infrastructure that could be cut by a foreign export restriction).

## Personal responsibility (SPR 110, 115)

Deputing work does not relieve an officer of responsibility. Contravention of SPR, FCs, or FSTBCM may attract discipline (SPR 110(a)). A person who is or was a public officer may be surcharged under Cap. 2 s.32. A CO who becomes aware of such circumstances must investigate and report as soon as practicable to [[pstsy]], with copies to the Secretary for the Civil Service, the Director of Accounting Services, and the Director of Audit (SPR 110(b)). Checks by DGL and the Director of Audit do not absolve any officer from complying within his own duties (SPR 115).

## Director of Government Logistics (SPR 120)

[[dgl]] advises departments on stores and procurement practice and runs compliance checks against prescribed criteria (SPR 120(a)). In that work DGL has access to all records, books, vouchers, documents, and receipts relating to procurement exercises and government stores in any public officer’s possession (SPR 120(b)). DGL may issue GLD Circulars and Circular Memoranda; with PS(Tsy) approval they have equal force to SPR (SPR 120(c)).

## Controlling Officers (SPR 125–135)

COs procure stores, services, and revenue contracts within the SPR limits and manage what is bought and awarded. They must observe SPR 106–109 and 109A, keep a culture of compliance, remind staff, and monitor (SPR 125). They may issue departmental instructions if the instructions comply with SPR and are **approved by DGL**; works and works-related consultancy instructions may also draw on DEVB TC(W). Build checks and balances and a clear segregation of roles; agree the segregation before tenders are invited; manage [[conflict-of-interest]] under Chapter IA (SPR 126). Observe [[wto-gpa]] where it applies (SPR 127). Keep stores properly, account for them, and inspect security regularly and by surprise (SPR 128). Reply promptly and fully to queries from the Director of Audit or DGL (SPR 130).

Appoint a [[dsm|Departmental Stores Manager]] to supervise procurement and stores management. Purchasing and stores functions should normally be done by Supplies Grade staff; COs must make enough of them available (SPR 135).

## Complaints (SPR 160)

Contractors, suppliers, firms, or organisations may complain about process or result to the procuring department, the relevant tender / consultants selection board, or the Office of The Ombudsman. Suspected corruption goes to the ICAC (SPR 160(a)). For [[wto-gpa]] procurements, a bid challenge may also go to the [[review-body|Review Body on Bid Challenges]] (SPR 160(b)).

COs must run an effective complaint system. On receipt or referral the CO must personally ensure an impartial, timely handling and a **substantive reply within ten working days** (interim reply if that is not possible). All complaints and substantive replies go to the relevant board (SPR 160(c) — *clause letter missing in the conversion; sequence is (a), (b), unnumbered paragraph, (d), (e)*).

If the CO was personally involved and an apparent conflict may arise, refer the complaint to the relevant board after departmental investigation; the CO or a designated directorate officer presents the file. The board decides the way forward (SPR 160(d)). If the board sees scope to change policy or system, it recommends to [[pstsy]], and for works-related items to the Permanent Secretary for Development (Works) (SPR 160(e)).
