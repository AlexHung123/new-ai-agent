# Chapter IA — Conflict of interest in government procurement

SPR 180–198 binds every public officer in a procurement, and every consulting firm or contractor whose roles may collide. It applies to **all types of government procurement, including revenue contracts, irrespective of value** (SPR 180). The concept page is [[conflict-of-interest]]. Drafting of consultancy clauses is a [[doj|Department of Justice]] matter (SPR 192, 196, 197). Chapter I policy sits on [[01-authority]]; consultant appointment on [[04-consultants]].

## Officers’ private interests (SPR 185–188)

Anyone involved in procurement — including the head, chair, members, and secretary of teams that write tender documents (specifications and marking schemes), tender opening teams, TAPs, [[dtc|DTCs]], DCSCs, tender boards, and consultants selection boards — must (SPR 185):

- avoid actual, potential, or perceived conflicts between official duties and private interests (the officer’s own, and those of relatives, close associates, or persons to whom the officer is indebted or owes a favour, as defined in CSB Circular No. 2/2004 or its successor);
- declare such conflicts as soon as aware, so a supervisor or chair can decide whether the officer stays on the exercise;
- follow prevailing civil-service conflict guidelines; and
- follow the Security Regulations: no unauthorised disclosure, and no taking advantage of tender-related information, whether or not for personal gain.

Officers who prepare tender documentation, assess tenders, or negotiate must declare on **taking up** those responsibilities and again as soon as they become aware of a conflict. Every tender report must state whether those officers declared, and what remedial action was taken if a conflict was identified. Specimen: Appendix I(A) (SPR 186).

Heads, chairs, members, and secretaries of opening teams, DTCs, DCSCs, tender boards, and consultants selection boards sign an undertaking on taking up the role and **renew it at least annually**. Specimen: Appendix I(B) (SPR 187).

[[controlling-officer|COs]] must regularly remind staff of confidentiality and declaration duties; ensure every declaration reaches the relevant chair (or the officer’s supervisor, if the declarant *is* the chair) and is filed; redeploy if the ruling is that the officer must step off; and, if appropriate, issue departmental supplements, referring to ICAC’s *Government Procurement of General Goods and Services* (SPR 188).

## Consultant then contractor (SPR 190–194)

A firm may hold conflicting roles on the same project or on related projects (SPR 190). The typical case is a firm that acted as the Government’s consultant and then bids as contractor — or is a controlling shareholder or subcontractor of that contractor — in the procurement that arose out of, or was the subject of, the consultancy (SPR 191).

To keep advice objective and the field level, departments must (SPR 192):

- before awarding a consultancy, ascertain so far as practicable whether a firm that has expressed interest should be disqualified for actual, potential, or perceived conflict;
- oblige the selected firm to report situations that may give rise to those conflicts; and
- **debar** the selected firm and its associate(s) from any subsequent procurement arising out of, or which was the very subject of, the consultancy, **save for SPR 194**.

Consult [[doj|D of J]] on the clauses that put SPR 192(b)–(c) into the brief and the agreement. If a department cannot comply with SPR 192, it must ask the relevant consultants selection board — or the tender board, if the professional advice was bought by tender — for a ruling (SPR 193).

### The SPR 194 exception, and the absolute ban

If the relevant consultants selection board accepts reasons for *not* debarring the former consultant (and/or its associates), the department must (SPR 194):

- review the consultant’s recommendations, including the proposed implementation specifications, so that the tender document is and is seen to be objective and unbiased. The CO appoints a **review committee of officers who were not involved in the consultancy** and who can serve as a credible check; record the decisions; and
- say in the implementation tender notices that the consultancy firm is a potential bidder, and that **all** information and relevant advice given to that firm will equally be made available to every potential tenderer on request.

**Absolute ban (SPR 194, last sentence).** Under no circumstances may a consulting firm (including its associates) that has **advised on the preparation of the tender document**, including specifications and marking scheme, bid, participate, or be financially involved in that or a related tender exercise. The review-committee route does not open this door.

## Equal opportunity and dual roles (SPR 195–196)

Where a firm’s people sit with the department on a committee, working group, or board out of which a consultancy assignment arises, the department must without delay either require that person to leave the body, or obtain a written undertaking that the firm will not compete for, participate in, or be financially involved in the assignment (as main or sub, or otherwise) (SPR 195(a)). Departments must not pressure firms to employ particular sub-consultants or over-match teams; if specialist-sub information is essential, put it in the EOI or tender invitation to **all** firms (SPR 195(b)).

A firm bidding as main consultant while also bidding as sub-consultant to a competitor — or teaming as sub-consultant with more than one main — creates a potential or perceived role conflict unless the roles are disclosed to all participants. Departments shall require disclosure of competing roles and a confirmation in the proposal that no confidential information, confidentiality restriction, or restraint of trade has been contravened where the main and/or sub is common or affiliated with another bidder’s sub. Consult D of J as necessary for the brief and agreement wording (SPR 196).

## Outside technical experts (SPR 197–198)

Advice from technical experts outside the Government (Appendix III(G)1, paragraph 3) is a special case. Before engagement, ascertain so far as practicable whether the expert should be disqualified for conflict; oblige the expert to report situations that may give rise to conflict (SPR 197). D of J may advise on the letter or agreement. Experts complete the declaration/undertaking at Appendix I(C).

If a conflict is identified, draw it to the attention of the TAP Chairman (Appendix III(G)1, paragraph 5). Document the action and report it to the DTC, DCSC, tender board, or consultants selection board as appropriate (SPR 198).
