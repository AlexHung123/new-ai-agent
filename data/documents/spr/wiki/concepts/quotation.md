# Quotation

Quotation is the Chapter II buying route used **at or below** the SPR 220 quotation limits. It is a value gate, not a judgement that the requirement is simple. Above those limits, departments follow [[tendering]] (Chapter III) or [[consultants-selection]] (Chapter IV) (SPR 221). A department may choose to tender or select consultants even inside the quotation limit; the Chapter III / IV procedures, including marking-scheme rules, then apply. See [[02-procurement]] and [[compare-procurement-routes]].

## The SPR 220 gate

| Type | Quotation limit | Departmental limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stores; services other than works; revenue contracts | $1.35 million | $10 million |
| Consultancy (general and works-related) | $3 million | $10 million |
| Construction and engineering works (UN CPC Division 51) | $7 million | $52 million for non-WTO GPA works under the simplified arrangement in FC 3/2009 |

Authority to buy up to those departmental limits by quotation or tendering has been delegated to [[controlling-officer|COs]] (SPR 220). Repeated purchases of the same item within 12 months must not push the cumulative value over the quotation limit (SPR 246).

## How a quotation is run (stores; services and revenue follow the same pattern)

- **≤ $50,000** — normally more than one quotation; accept the lowest conforming offer or the highest overall score if a [[marking-scheme]] is used. Contact by an officer not below Supplies Supervisor II or equivalent; accept by not below Assistant Supplies Officer / Executive Officer II or equivalent (SPR 260(a), 280, 295).
- **Over $50,000 up to the quotation limit** — at least five written quotations, unless a higher-ranked officer authorises fewer (SPR 260(b), (d)). Written quotations include email, fax, or post.
- Half-yearly returns of purchases over $50,000 go to [[dgl|DGL]]. Purchases exceeding $500,000 need an explanation if fewer than five written quotations were received, or if a higher-priced / non-highest-score offer is accepted (SPR 260(c)).

Rank tables for inviting and accepting quotations are in SPR 260(d) and 260(g) (and the service / revenue counterparts). Do not treat those ranks as optional.

The officer who approves formal orders is not below Assistant Supplies Officer / Executive Officer II unless [[dgl|DGL]] agrees, and reports to a more senior [[dsm|DSM]] (SPR 255). Only in exceptional circumstances, with DSM clearance, may a department buy before placing a formal order.

## Marking schemes on quotations

Departments may use a marking scheme on a quotation. Works and consultancy schemes need directorate approval before invitation. For stores, non-works services, and revenue, a scheme that **deviates** from the SMS Framework needs directorate approval before invitation (SPR 221A). Tenderer’s experience should not be an essential requirement in non-works, non-consultancy buys.

Sale of goods for disposal of government stores or confiscated goods is done by GLD, not as a departmental revenue quotation (SPR 220 Note 1). See [[disposal]].
