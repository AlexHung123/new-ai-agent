# Value for money

Government procurement policy is to obtain stores and services at the best value for money in a publicly accountable manner, to support Government programmes and activities (SPR 106). That policy sits at the front of Chapter I and is binding on every route — [[quotation]], [[tendering]], [[consultants-selection]], or [[direct-engagement]]. See [[01-authority]].

## Not the lowest price

“Value for money” does not simply mean buying at the lowest price (SPR 107). It is assessed in terms of economy, effectiveness, and efficiency, taking into account:

- total costs on a **whole-life costing** basis; and
- the overall value created or brought about by the procurement.

In other words, it is the optimal use of resources to achieve the best outcome.

## Public value, not only cash

[[controlling-officer|Controlling Officers]] must ensure public funds are well spent in terms of the total **positive value** the procurement creates. Positive values may include (but are not limited to) social, environmental, economic, and/or other values that serve the public interest (SPR 107).

COs must also ask whether the procurement is more costly than necessary. They should be satisfied that the buy is genuinely required and justified on legitimate and defensible grounds — for example, essential operational need and/or better value for money. Departments should normally not disclose the estimated contract value, because it can become a guiding factor that inflates or shrinks bids and undermines competition (SPR 347(a), 445(a)).

## How it shows up later in SPR

- A recommendation to a tender board or [[dtc|DTC]] must have value for money in mind. Close tendered sums, or payments over several years, are compared on a present-value basis (SPR 370(f)).
- A [[marking-scheme]] exists so that technical quality and innovation can outweigh a raw price rank. Lowest conforming is the default only when price is the sole criterion (SPR 380(a)).
- Direct purchase from a department instead of through [[dgl|GLD]], even when GLD holds stock or has a bulk contract, needs DGL consent “having regard to value for money” (SPR 245).

COs remain accountable for the purchasing decision (SPR 108). Value for money is applied together with the four [[procurement-principles]] in SPR 109 and the [[national-security]] duty in SPR 109A. It is not a licence to skip open bidding: a higher-priced or non-highest-score quotation still needs the higher-rank column in SPR 260(g) / 280(g), and a tender that is not the lowest / highest overall scorer is reported monthly to the next board (SPR 310(d), (g)).
