# Conflict of interest

Chapter IA applies to all government procurement, including revenue contracts, irrespective of value (SPR 180). Every public officer in the process must watch for **actual, potential, or perceived** conflict and put safeguards in place. Conflicts can arise from an officer’s private interests, or from the different roles a consulting firm or contractor plays. See [[01a-conflict-of-interest]].

## Officers

Private interests include the financial and other interests of the officer, the officer’s relatives and close associates, or persons to whom the officer is indebted or owes a favour (CSB Circular 2/2004 or any update) (SPR 185(a)).

Officers who prepare tender documentation (including specifications and [[marking-scheme|marking schemes]]), assess tenders, or negotiate must declare on taking up the role and again as soon as they become aware of a conflict (SPR 186). The tender report must state whether they declared and, if a conflict was identified, what was done. Specimen: Appendix I(A).

Heads, chairs, members, and secretaries of tender-opening teams, [[dtc|DTCs]], DCSCs, tender boards, and consultants selection boards sign an undertaking on taking up the role and renew it at least annually (SPR 187; Appendix I(B)).

If the officer who declared is told not to continue, the [[controlling-officer|CO]] redeploys staff (SPR 188). Unauthorised disclosure of tender information is also forbidden (SPR 185(d)).

## Firms — the absolute ban

A typical risk is a firm that advised Government on a project then bidding as contractor, controlling shareholder, or subcontractor on the implementation (SPR 191). Departments must, so far as practicable, check for conflict before awarding a consultancy, oblige the selected firm to report later conflicts, and **debar** that firm and its associates from the subsequent procurement arising out of, or which was the very subject of, the consultancy — save SPR 194 (SPR 192).

If the relevant [[ccsb|consultants selection board]] accepts that a strict ban is not practicable, the department must (SPR 194):

- appoint a review committee of officers who were **not** involved in the consultancy, review the recommendations and specifications so the tender document is and is seen to be objective; and
- say in the implementation tender notice that the consultant is a potential bidder, and that all information and advice given to that firm will be equally available to all tenderers on request.

**Under no circumstances** may a consulting firm (including associates) that advised on the preparation of the tender document — including specifications and marking scheme — bid, participate, or be financially involved in that or a related tender (SPR 194, last sentence). There is no SPR 194 carve-out for that fact pattern.

## Other firm-role rules

A person from a consulting firm sitting on a government committee when a new assignment arises must leave the committee or undertake that the firm will not compete (SPR 195). Departments must not pressure firms to employ particular sub-consultants. Competing main/sub-consultant roles in the same exercise must be disclosed (SPR 196). Outside technical experts declare at Appendix I(C); identified conflicts go to the TAP chair and are reported to the [[dtc|DTC]], DCSC, tender board, or consultants selection board (SPR 197–198). Consult [[doj]] on the contractual wording. COs remind staff at regular intervals and may issue departmental supplements, referring to the ICAC “Government Procurement of General Goods and Services” booklet (SPR 188).
