# Procurement principles

SPR 106–107 state the policy ([[value-for-money]] in a publicly accountable manner). SPR 109 underpins that policy with four principles. They apply to all government procurement, including revenue contracts. See [[01-authority]].

## (a) Open and fair competition

Competition is treated as a safeguard against overcharging and against holding Government to ransom. Open bidding is the **norm** (SPR 109(a)(ii)). Single/restricted [[tendering]] or [[direct-engagement]] of suppliers, service providers, or consultants is the exception and must be justified to the relevant approving authority.

All bidders are treated on an equal footing. There is no discrimination on country of origin. Requirements, specifications, and [[marking-scheme|marking schemes]] must be drawn up objectively. For exercises that had limited competition last time, departments should look for ways to attract new bidders and innovative proposals (SPR 109(a)(iv)).

## (b) Transparency

Potential bidders receive the same information. Departments publish tender notices for all open tenders, and invitations for admission to supplier / service-provider lists, on their webpages. Tender documents and consultancy briefs should be clear, concise, and easy to understand so that bidding is not made unnecessarily costly (SPR 109(b)).

## (c) Pro-innovation

Innovation is treated as a long-run value-for-money tool. Departments should be receptive to new ideas and should use outcome-based requirements. Over-specification and over-prescription are to be avoided (SPR 109(c)).

For stores, services (excluding works and consultancy), and revenue contracts, “innovation” includes:

- technology-related innovation (new technology, or innovative use of existing technology); and
- non-technology ideas not conventionally adopted that create public value — for example cleaner streets, environmental protection, employment of persons with disability, or user-centric design.

A minimum share of technical marks for innovative suggestions is reserved under the SMS Framework (FC 2/2019; see [[marking-scheme]]).

## (d) Integrity

Procurement decisions are made impartially and public funds are spent accountably (SPR 109(d)). That principle is operationalised in Chapter IA ([[conflict-of-interest]]) and in the duty of public officers at SPR 110.

These four principles sit beside the continuing [[national-security]] duty (SPR 109A). [[controlling-officer|COs]] must observe SPR 106–109 and 109A when conducting any procurement, regardless of value, means, or type (SPR 205A). Specifications must not create unnecessary obstacles to international trade or to competition (SPR 350(a)). Experience should not be an essential requirement in non-works, non-consultancy buys unless it is genuinely needed and is not imposed to avoid competition (SPR 221A, 350).
