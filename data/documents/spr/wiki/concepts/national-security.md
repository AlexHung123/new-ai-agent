# Duty to safeguard national security

It is the constitutional duty of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region to safeguard national security. Executive authorities, including all bureaux and departments, must prevent and suppress any act or activity endangering national security (SPR 109A(a)). In procurement, [[controlling-officer|COs]] take that duty into account and must not engage in acts or activities that endanger national security. See [[01-authority]].

## A continuing duty at every stage

The duty subsists throughout the procurement: before award and after, including whether to approve sub-contracting, conditions of approval, and ongoing monitoring of contractors and approved sub-contractors (SPR 109A(b)). SPR 205A repeats that officers must have regard to SPR 106–109 and 109A **regardless of procurement value, means, or type**. National-security issues are brought to directorate officers at the earliest opportunity; those officers supervise the response.

COs use professional judgement, the highest degree of sensitivity, and prudence. Note 1 to SPR 109A requires a critical review, on publicly available or other information (including information required from tenderers), of tenderers’ background and track record — including whether they have engaged or are reasonably believed to be engaging in acts likely to constitute or cause offences endangering national security, whether they are subject to sanctions by the People’s Republic of China, and any affiliation with a government, entity, or organisation that may pose a national-security risk. The nature of the buy also matters (for example maintenance of critical infrastructure that could stop because of a foreign export restriction). The review is required regardless of value, means, or type.

## Standard contract clauses

Standard forms at SPR 345(a)(iv)–(vi) include clauses that allow the Government to **disqualify** a tenderer and to **terminate** the contract in the interest of national security. Departments **shall** incorporate them. If they consider the clauses unsuitable, they seek prior clearance from the relevant policy bureau and [[doj|D of J]] and/or LAD(W)/DEVB (SPR 345).

## Restricted routes

- Single or restricted [[tendering]] may be justified where, in the interest of national security, the stores, services, or revenue contract could not be procured by open or selective tender (SPR 325(a)(viii)).
- [[direct-engagement]] may be used where those tender types would not be effective, for example in the interest of national security, extreme urgency, or exclusive rights (SPR 331).

## Escalation and publication

Specific national-security concerns go first to the policy bureau; the bureau may ask the Security Bureau. D of J / LAD(W)/DEVB and [[dgl|GLD]] are consulted on legal and procedural points at the earliest opportunity (SPR 109A(c)). Publishing a contract-award notice may be withheld, after legal advice and policy support, with [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]] approval, if publication would be contrary to national security or the public interest of the HKSAR (SPR 390(c)).

Tender evaluation (SPR 370) is not a national-security holiday: the TAP still scores against the published scheme, but the continuing duty in SPR 109A(b) runs through assessment, sub-contracting approval, and contract management. The duty applies to a $50,000 quotation as much as to a CTB tender.
