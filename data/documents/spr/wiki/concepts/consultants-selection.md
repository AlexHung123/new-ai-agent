# Consultants selection

Chapter IV is used to obtain services from **consulting firms** when tender procedures are not applicable — typically where Government lacks the expertise, the end-product depends on the firm’s experience and method, the qualities cannot be precisely specified, or selection on price alone would lower quality (SPR 400). It is not used for consultancies within the SPR 220(a) [[quotation]] limit ($3 million), employment of individuals, or briefing-out by the Secretary for Justice (SPR 405). See [[04-consultants]].

## Gate and who appoints

Unless delegated, [[pstsy|PS(Tsy)]] appoints consulting firms (SPR 410). Before proposing an appointment the department must have:

- policy support from the relevant Director of Bureau (or an authorised officer);
- confirmation that qualified staff are not available in the department, elsewhere in Government, or within the required timeframe;
- MA Division of FSTB endorsement for financial consultancies (or financial sub-consultants);
- Digital Policy Office support for general management consultancies.

[[sfst|SFST]] has delegated selection and appointment up to the $10 million departmental limit to [[controlling-officer|COs]], exercised through a DCSC (SPR 426). Above that, [[ccsb|CCSB]] advises PS(Tsy), except architectural work (AACSB) and engineering work (EACSB), which approve in their fields with no dollar limit (SPR 116, 425).

## Two-stage CCSB process

- **Stage 1** — long list; optional EOI (normally ≥ two weeks); assessment panel of government officials; consultancy brief cleared with [[doj|D of J]] as needed; [[marking-scheme]] designed (technical usually 60–70%); invite proposals from the recommended firms (SPR 430(a), 440).
- **Stage 2** — issue the approved brief (normally ≥ three weeks); **two envelopes** (Technical Proposal / Fee Proposal); score technical first, then fees; recommend the highest overall scorer; negotiating brief and a fallback firm (SPR 445).

Direct invitation of proposals (skipping EOI) is allowed where expertise is scarce, the market is unknown, or time is short (SPR 440(b)) — that is still a competitive list, not a one-firm approach.

## Direct one-firm approach

Approaching a single firm without competition is reserved to PS(Tsy) and is **not** delegated to COs at any value (SPR 430(b)). After that approval the department still puts a combined Stages 1 and 2 paper to CCSB or DCSC. See [[direct-engagement]].

[[parallel-tendering]] applies: mandatory risk-assessed default for CWRF works-related consultancies; optional for others (SPR 416). Communications until execution are RESTRICTED (CONTRACT) (SPR 420). Watch [[conflict-of-interest]], especially the absolute ban in SPR 194 if the firm drafted the tender document.

Unless PS(Tsy) authorises otherwise, the officer who signs the consultancy agreement is at directorate level (SPR 460). Fees may be time charges (normally with a ceiling), a lump sum, or both (SPR 465). Cancellation of a live exercise needs the board or DCSC, except where no proposals arrive or parallel-tendering funding fails (SPR 470).
