# Marking scheme

A marking scheme scores technical quality and price so that award is not only lowest (or highest, for revenue) conforming price. Departments are encouraged to use one so there is room to assess innovative suggestions (SPR 350). Schemes must be clear, objective, and commensurate with the nature, scale, and value of the buy. Guidelines are at Appendix III(G). See [[03-tenders-process]] and [[value-for-money]].

## Standard Marking Scheme Framework (non-works)

For non-works tenders, departments follow the SMS Framework in FC 2/2019 (as updated) (SPR 350):

- technical weighting normally **50–70%**, price **30–50%**;
- a minimum percentage of technical marks is reserved for innovative suggestions;
- no prior approval from the tender board or [[dtc|DTC]] if the scheme stays inside the Framework;
- a [[controlling-officer|CO]] may designate a directorate officer to approve technical weighting **below 50%**, only where the goods or services are simple and significant technical variation is unlikely;
- technical weighting **above 70%**, or any other deviation (for example leaving the permitted mark bands), needs the board or DTC **before invitation**;
- for service contracts that rely heavily on non-skilled workers, technical weighting may **not** go below 50%, and “wages” may not be given fewer than 25 of 100 technical marks.

COs designate officers (normally directorate) to approve a non-works tender that will **not** use a marking scheme.

## Works and consultancy

For works tenders, use of a marking scheme or deviation from the DEVB standard scheme needs the relevant tender board unless a specific delegation or a standard scheme in DEVB TC(W) already covers it (SPR 350).

All consultancy marking schemes are approved by the relevant [[ccsb|consultants selection board or DCSC]]. Technical weighting is normally around **60–70%**, unless that board is satisfied otherwise (SPR 427, 440(g)).

On a [[quotation]], works and consultancy schemes need directorate approval before invitation; stores / non-works services / revenue schemes that deviate from SMS need the same (SPR 221A).

## Two-envelope rule

Because a marking scheme separates technical evaluation from the tendered sum, a **two-envelope** approach is used (Appendix III(G) para. 12). Technical and price information go in separately marked sealed envelopes. Price envelopes are not normally opened until technical assessment is finished (SPR 445(c)–(d) for consultancies; same idea for tenders). Separate TAP teams for technical and price may be used (Appendix III(G)1).

Tender documents must outline the criteria, individual technical marks, any passing marks, the scoring formula, and the technical-to-price weighting (SPR 350). The TAP recommends the tender that attains any passing marks and the highest overall score (SPR 370(c)). If none of the tenders conform or attain the passing marks, the exercise is cancelled and retendered; recommending a non-conforming tender is exceptional and needs [[doj|D of J]] or LAD(W)/DEVB clearance (SPR 370(g)). On re-tender, departments review specifications, essential requirements, and the scheme to encourage competition (SPR 350(m)).
