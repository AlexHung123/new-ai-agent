# Synthesis — what SPR is actually regulating

SPR is not a purchasing manual. It is a **delegation machine** sitting on the Public Finance Ordinance: it says who may spend, who may except, who must be seen to compete, and how stores remain on charge until they are written off or dumped. The wiki’s job is to make the delegation graph readable.

## 1. PS(Tsy) is the default; everything else is a carve-out

Unless a regulation names another officer, [[pstsy]] accepts tenders, appoints consultants, authorises negotiations, varies contracts, writes off high-value losses, and excepts the rules (SPR 310, 385, 390, 410, 430(b), 520, 1070). [[dgl]], [[controlling-officer|COs]], [[dtc|DTCs]], [[ctb|subsidiary boards]], and [[ccsb|DCSCs]] exist only inside published dollar and subject limits. If a page does not say who approves, the answer is PS(Tsy).

## 2. Value-for-money is not lowest price

[[value-for-money]] is economy + effectiveness + efficiency on a whole-life basis, including social, environmental and other public value (SPR 107). That is why [[marking-scheme|marking schemes]] (technical 50–70% under the SMS Framework) and the pro-innovation principle sit in the same chapter as the quotation tables. A “lowest conforming” recommendation is the default only when no marking scheme is used (SPR 380(a), 370(c)).

## 3. Restricted routes are exceptions with a paper trail

[[tendering|Open tender]] is the norm (SPR 109, 315). [[direct-engagement]], single/restricted tender, and a no-competition consultant approach all require directorate endorsement and a named approving officer — often the CO personally below the departmental limit, otherwise PS(Tsy), with DGL in the loop for stores (SPR 325, 331–333, 430(b)). National security, extreme urgency, and exclusive rights are the usual justifications; “to avoid competition” is expressly forbidden.

## 4. Conflict rules bind both officers and firms

Chapter IA is not courtesy. Officers declare on taking up a role and again when they notice a conflict (SPR 186–187). A consultant who wrote the specification **cannot** bid the implementation, with no exception (SPR 194 last sentence). A consultant who only did the study may bid later only if a review committee re-writes the specification and all advice is shared (SPR 194). This is why [[conflict-of-interest]] appears on almost every process page.

## 5. Stores accounting is a second statute inside the same book

Chapters VI–XII are a property regime: two classes of stores, a $1,000 inventory line, personal custody, surcharge under Cap. 2 s.32, and a disposal tree that prefers return to GLD → other departments → donation / sale → dump (SPR 700–710, 885, 1080, 1110–1151). Procurement staff who never open Chapter VII still create inventory the moment a $1,000+ item is received.

## 6. WTO GPA and national security cut across every route

[[wto-gpa]] changes notice periods (40 / 25 days), limited-tender grounds, and record-keeping. [[national-security]] changes who you may deal with and when you must stop, at any value. Neither is a “large-contract only” overlay. SPR 205A and 109A say so in terms.

## 7. The compilation is not the gazetted text

`MinerU_markdown_spr_e_clean.md` drops SPR 116 and 1110 to images (transcribed on [[ctb]] and [[11-surplus-disposal]]), glues headings, and breaks some tables (notably SPR 362 and 1070). Wiki pages reconstruct the readable rule and flag residual gaps. For a live purchase, a surcharge, or a bid challenge, use the current FSTB / GLD text, not this wiki.
