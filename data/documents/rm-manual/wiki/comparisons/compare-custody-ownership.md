# Compare — custody versus ownership when records leave Government

Inside Government, records of a transferred function move with control records and must **not** be top-numbered ([[07-administrative-changes]]). Outside Government, nothing moves until the [[grs-director]] says so (RMM 822). Concept: [[custody-ownership]]. Chapter: [[07-outsourcing-transfer]].

| Arrangement | Physical holding | Legal / intellectual rights | Still a government record? | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Transfer of custody** (RMM 824) | Receiver (quasi-government or private) | **Stay with Government** | **Yes** — still subject to government regulatory requirements | Day-to-day management moves; Government keeps title |
| **Transfer of ownership** (RMM 825) | Receiver | **All relinquished** | **No** — no longer subject to in-house Government requirements | Only with GRS Director authority |
| **Outsourcing default** (RMM 826) | May sit with contractor | **Normally Government** | **Yes** — B/D must make the contractor meet recordkeeping requirements | Service delivery on Government’s behalf |
| **Copies to the new organisation** (RMM 829(a)) | Both | Government keeps originals | Originals yes | Meet the new organisation’s need without handing over title |
| **Temporary custody, return after an agreed period** (RMM 829(b)) | Receiver, then back | Government | Yes | Time-limited operational need |
| **Access while Government keeps custody** (RMM 829(c)) | Government | Government | Yes | New organisation consults; no physical transfer |

Every extra-government option still needs: specification in the sale/contract/ordinance (or a decision **not** to include records there), prior GRS concurrence, and a documented agreement **endorsed by the GRS Director** (RMM 821–823). GRS’s role is to keep records needed by Government or important to the community **under Government ownership** (RMM 827).

RMM 824 is printed as `24.` in the conversion.
