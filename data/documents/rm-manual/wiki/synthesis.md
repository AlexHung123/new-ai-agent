# Synthesis — what the Records Management Manual is actually regulating

The Manual is not an archives act and not a volume of the Government Regulations. It is a **Director of Administration code of practices** that tells every B/D to run one records programme, share the load with [[grs]], and treat records as evidence of official business. The wiki’s job is to make that programme readable — not to replace GARDS, the Security Regulations, or the Code on Access to Information.

## 1. Evidence is the definition, not paper

[[government-records]] are recorded information created or received in official business **and kept as evidence** of policies, decisions, procedures, functions, activities and transactions (RMM 115). Evidence requires **content, structure and context** inside a [[recordkeeping-system]] (RMM 116–119). That is why extra copies for convenience are non-records, why a complete capture must show who wrote it, for whom, when, and how it links to other records (RMM 313), and why a “Post-it” or thermal-paper note is not a way to add permanent information (RMM 316). Format is secondary: [[electronic-records]] are government records (RMM 123).

## 2. Shared responsibility, one destruction key

B/Ds create and keep; GRS is the central agency (RMM 201). The [[head-of-bd]] owns the programme; the [[drm]] runs it; GRS writes standards, trains, inspects, and keeps the archives (RMM 209–220). The structural sentence is RMM 605: **no government records are to be destroyed without the prior agreement of the [[grs-director]]**. Disposal schedules for [[programme-records]] are authorised through the [[rmo]]; [[administrative-records]] follow GARDS (RMM 612–615). Unauthorised removal, defacing, alteration, destruction or loss is reported to the GRS Director immediately (RMM 606). That is the integrity bar.

## 3. Administrative versus programme is the filing *and* disposal fork

[[administrative-records]] document common internal services (finance, accommodation, procurement, establishment, personnel). They use GRS’s subject scheme and GARDS (RMM 418–419, 611–612). [[programme-records]] document the mission the B/D was established to perform; they are unique; the B/D builds the scheme and the schedules, then gets GRS Director authority (RMM 417, 613–615). Mixing the two paths is how offices either over-retain clutter or destroy the wrong series. See [[compare-admin-vs-programme]].

## 4. Access is two clocks, and classification is not a refusal

Current records: the **Code on Access to Information** — identify holdings, answer in 10 days (30 if complicated), publish categories; a security stamp is **not by itself** a valid refusal (RMM 461–464). Archives: the **Public Records (Access) Rules** — unclassified archival records reaching 30 years and held by [[pro]] are released automatically; classified ones are reviewed by the creating B/D; the public may apply for closed records regardless of grade; the GRS Director may permit inspection of closed archives or withhold for preservation (RMM 465–471). Personal data has its own ordinance path (RMM 472). See [[records-access]].

## 5. When a function leaves, records do not just follow the people

Inside Government, the function-losing B/D transfers related records **in their entirety with control records**, and the inheritor **must not top-number** them into a new series (RMM 810–814). Outside Government, nothing goes into a sale, contract or ordinance unless the GRS Director has issued authority to transfer [[custody-ownership|custody or ownership]] (RMM 822). Custody keeps Government legal title; ownership relinquishes it. Outsourced service records normally stay Government-owned, and the contractor must meet recordkeeping requirements (RMM 824–826). See [[compare-custody-ownership]].

## 6. Vital records are a short, approved list — not “everything important”

[[vital-records]] are what the B/D needs **during and after** an emergency (RMM 700). Three grades: essential (emergency operations), important (legal/financial status), useful (rights of the public) (RMM 706). Designation is highly selective, by the DRM with head or deputy-head approval (RMM 710). Finding aids, and for electronic media the software and contextual documentation, travel with the set (RMM 707–709). Disposal of time-expired vital records follows Chapter 6 like any other record (RMM 730).

## 7. This conversion is a 2001/2020 snapshot, not the live GRS website

RMM 107 still says a strategy for electronic information systems “will be issued in due course”. RMM 717 still names the Information Technology Services Department. Publications in Appendix A are 2000-vintage titles (GARDS, Subject Filing, Vital Records Protection). The Manual tells B/Ds to follow it “as far as possible” (RMM 103) while planting hard bars on destruction and alienation. For a live decision, use current GRS circulars and publications, Security Regulations, and the Code — not this wiki alone.
