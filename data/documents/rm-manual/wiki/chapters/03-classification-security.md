# Security classification and downgrading

RMM 430–449: four Security Regulations grades, and a five-year DRM review to take stamps off when they are no longer needed. ## Four categories (RMM 430–433)

Records are classified according to their **level of sensitivity at a particular time** (RMM 430). Four categories of classified information (RMM 431):

1. Top Secret
2. Secret
3. Confidential
4. Restricted

Definitions and examples: **Security Regulations** — not restated here (RMM 432).

A document should **not** be given a particular grade purely because it refers to other documents so classified, or because previous papers on that subject received that classification, or because of the classification of the record on which it was filed (RMM 433).

## Downgrading (RMM 440–443)

A security restriction should be imposed for a **stated period** so special handling lasts no longer than necessary (RMM 440). Consider downgrading as soon as the need for special protection diminishes (RMM 441).

Classified documents should **not** be downgraded without reference to the **originator** and the agency or operation on which the originator’s functions have devolved (RMM 442).

The [[drm]] should review classified documents and records in custody **at least every 5 years** to downgrade suitable items, adhering to the Security Regulations and coordinating with agencies that have a direct interest in the subjects (RMM 443).
