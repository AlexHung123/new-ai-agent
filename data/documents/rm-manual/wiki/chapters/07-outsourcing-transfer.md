# Outsourcing, privatisation, corporatisation, and personal data

RMM 821–838: when a function leaves Government, records do not automatically follow. Custody and ownership are different, and the [[grs-director]] is the transfer key. Comparison: [[compare-custody-ownership]]. Intra-government moves: [[07-administrative-changes]]. Concept: [[custody-ownership]].

## Agreement first (RMM 821–823)

Where functions are outsourced, transferred or sold to the private sector, the agreement should specify custody, ownership and use of records, which types remain government records, and final disposition. **Prior concurrence of [[grs]]** should be obtained (RMM 821).

**No records should be included in any legislation, contract of sale or agreement** in respect of privatisation or transfer of a function outside the Government unless an authority to transfer custody or ownership has been issued by the GRS Director (RMM 822).

Any such transfer should be adequately documented. A documented agreement between the transferring B/D and the new custodian/owner should be made and **endorsed by the GRS Director** (RMM 823).

## Custody versus ownership (RMM 824–826)

**Transfer of custody:** day-to-day management moves to a quasi-government or private organisation. The Government **retains intellectual and other property rights**; the receiving organisation holds the records physically and manages their use. Legal ownership does **not** change; the records remain subject to government regulatory requirements (RMM 824).

**Transfer of ownership:** the Government relinquishes **all legal, physical and intellectual rights**. The records are no longer subject to in-house regulatory or administrative requirements of the Government (RMM 825).

When a B/D outsources delivery of services, **ownership of records arising from the services or contract-supporting services is normally vested with the Government**. The B/D must ensure the private party meets government [[recordkeeping-system|recordkeeping]] requirements (RMM 826).

## GRS’s role and alternatives (RMM 827–830)

Inform GRS **in advance** for authorisation and appropriate disposal. GRS’s role is to ensure records needed by the Government or important to the community **remain under Government ownership** (RMM 827). The transferring B/D recommends future custody and ownership and any conditions (RMM 828).

Sometimes, to meet the new organisation’s needs, it may be appropriate to (RMM 829):

- make **copies** available;
- arrange **temporary transfer of custody** with return after an agreed period; or
- allow **access** while the Government keeps custody.

Detailed requirements: Records Management Publication No. 2 — *Managing Active Records* (RMM 830).

## Employment-related personal data (RMM 836–838)

When functions are outsourced, privatised or corporatised, employment-related personal data collected for purposes directly related to employment may be transferred to the non-government agencies. Take practical steps to protect them from unauthorised or accidental access or disclosure (RMM 836).

Transfers between B/Ds on posting or redeployment should be documented, with adequate security against loss, damage, unauthorised alteration and access (RMM 837). Observe the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance and the mandatory provisions of the Privacy Commissioner’s Code of Practice on Human Resource Management (RMM 838).
