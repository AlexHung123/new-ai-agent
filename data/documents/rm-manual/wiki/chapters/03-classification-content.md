## Scheme (RMM 414–416)

Records classification is the systematic arrangement of records according to a method or system, often by content, to organise related papers and support retrieval (RMM 414). A classification scheme is a plan for logical arrangement according to intellectual content, physical formats or other criteria to **reflect the programmes and functions** of the organisation (RMM 415). Each B/D should establish and maintain a scheme that builds around its functions and organisation and **covers all official records** (RMM 416).

## Two families (RMM 417–419)

**Programme or function records** are created or received while carrying out the **primary functions, activities or missions** for which the agency was established. They are unique to each B/D. B/Ds should develop their **own** classification scheme. How-to: Records Management Publication No. 3 — *Subject Filing* (RMM 417).

**Administrative records** document common service activities relating to **internal administration**. B/Ds should **adopt** the GRS subject classification scheme in Publication No. 3 (RMM 418). That scheme gives government-wide uniformity and feeds disposal sentences in Publication No. 4 — *General Administrative Records Disposal Schedules* ([[records-scheduling|GARDS]]) (RMM 419).

## Proper-scheme tests (RMM 420)

A proper scheme should:

- show linkages between individual records to provide a continuous record of activities;
- uniquely name and code records consistently over time;
- properly determine security protection and appropriate access;
- be supported by vocabulary control suited to the complexity of the records; and
- be current, comprehensive, logical, consistent and structured.

## Inventory and upkeep (RMM 421–424)

Incorporate the most current records in a timely manner (RMM 421). Prepare and maintain an **inventory** of all records titles, status (active or inactive) and locations (RMM 422). Regularly review the scheme and inventory for changes and new needs (RMM 423). Written procedures for coding, classifying, indexing, retrieving and updating; records staff trained in them (RMM 424).
