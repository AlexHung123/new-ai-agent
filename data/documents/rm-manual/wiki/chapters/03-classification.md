# Recordkeeping system, records versus non-records, tracking

## Recordkeeping system (RMM 400–403)

Records should be identifiable, retrievable, accessible and available using systems that verify their existence and let them be found quickly (RMM 400). Organise, classify, describe and control them with **unique numbers (and/or letters) and titles** (RMM 401).

A [[recordkeeping-system]] is a manual or automated information system in which records are collected, organised and categorised to facilitate retrieval, distribution, use, disposal or preservation. It should include **at least** (RMM 402):

- a records classification scheme(s) with related index(es);
- a complete records inventory;
- records disposal schedules;
- vital records lists; and
- procedures and documentation for coding, filing, retrieval, movement tracking, disposal, and identification and protection of vital records.

General principles: Records Management Publication No. 2 — *Managing Active Records: File Management* (RMM 403).

## Records versus non-records (RMM 404–406)

Maintain only materials of **record status (official record copy)** in the recordkeeping system (RMM 404). Definition of government records: RMM 115–117.

Record status does **not** depend on unique information. Copies of the same document have record status if they are created or received to document official functions, policies, decisions, operations, procedures and transactions **and kept as evidence** (RMM 405).

Non-record materials may include (RMM 406):

- library and museum material acquired solely for reference or exhibition;
- stocks of publications and blank or obsolete forms;
- extra copies generated for convenience or reference;
- drafting materials or working papers, **on condition that the official record copy is properly filed**; and
- routing slips, etc.

## Personal papers (RMM 410–413)

[[personal-papers]] are documentary materials of a **private nature that do not relate to or have an effect upon** the conduct of official business. They are excluded from the definition of government records (RMM 410). Examples: materials accumulated before joining the service and not later used in government business; materials relating solely to private affairs that do not require declaration; diaries, journals, personal correspondence or notes **not** prepared, used, circulated or communicated in the course of government business.

Personal papers should be clearly designated and **at all times separated** from official records (RMM 411). If private matters and official business appear in the **same document**, copy it and treat the copy as an official record at receipt, with personal information deleted; with the officer’s agreement the original may be put on file (RMM 412).

Materials labelled “personal”, “confidential”, “private” or similarly, **and used in the transaction of government business, are official records** and are managed and disposed of as such (RMM 413).

## Tracking (RMM 480–482)

Maintain an audit trail of transactions and be able to identify the operational origin of individual records where systems have been amalgamated or migrated (RMM 480). Document physical movement so items can always be located (RMM 481). Whether manual or barcode, the system should control issue between persons, return to home location, and disposal or transfer to authorised bodies (RMM 482).
