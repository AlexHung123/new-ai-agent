## Authority (RMM 600–606)

Appraise creation, retention and disposal systematically against business need, public accountability, regulatory requirements, resources, and public expectation that some records will be preserved (RMM 600).

**Disposal is not destruction only.** It is the action taken when a record is no longer in active use: permanent retention; retention for a specified period before destruction; transfer into other media (microfilm, optical discs); or destroying the records (RMM 601).

[[grs]] has overall responsibility for authorising disposal by approving disposal **requests** and **schedules**, and issuing disposal authority or agreement (RMM 602). How-to: Publication No. 1 — *A Practical Guide to Records Scheduling and Disposal* (revised and retitled, 2000) (RMM 603).

Development of schedules and the disposal decision should be exercised by the [[drm]] as approved by the [[head-of-bd]] (RMM 604).

**No government records are to be destroyed without the prior agreement of the [[grs-director]]** (RMM 605). Unauthorised removal, defacing, alteration, destruction or loss should be reported to the GRS Director **immediately** (RMM 606).

## Scheduling (RMM 610–620)

Scheduling develops retention and disposal schedules: how long to keep, how to dispose (RMM 610). Procedures differ for administrative and programme records (RMM 611). See [[compare-admin-vs-programme]].

- **Administrative records** — follow Publication No. 4, GARDS (RMM 612).
- **Programme records** — B/Ds develop their own schedules (RMM 613) and **must obtain disposal authority** from the GRS Director through the [[rmo]] (RMM 615).

All changes to schedules should be agreed with the GRS Director; lodge amendments with RMO first (RMM 616). Review all schedules **at least every 5 years** (RMM 617). Where practicable, assign definite retention periods **before** any disposal action (RMM 618). Permanent retention **in a B/D** is usually valid only when required by legislation (RMM 619). Keep a master set of schedules and authorities plus documentation of what was transferred, destroyed or migrated (RMM 620).

## Disposal actions (RMM 625–632)

Dispose through: permanent retention; transfer to other media before destroying the original; transfer of inactive records to off-site storage; or immediate destruction when they have no retention value (RMM 625). Records appraised as archival by the Archivist of PRO should be transferred to and preserved by PRO (RMM 626). Dispose only in accordance with agreed schedules or separate GRS Director approval; records should not otherwise be destroyed or **removed from the control of the Government** (RMM 627). Avoid **ad hoc** disposal (RMM 628).

Use [[records-centre]] facilities operated by RMO for inactive records as far as practicable (RMM 629). Destruction of Records Centre holdings is **not** arranged unless the transferring B/D consents in writing; if the B/D fails to respond within **3 months** after the records are due, RMO **reserves the right to destroy** them (RMM 630).

To dispose of library collections or printed materials, contact the **Central Preservation Library for Government Publications** (an integral part of PRO). Send a copy of newly published reports, books, posters or other printed material to the Library unless otherwise specified (General Circular 1/2001). The Library selects items that warrant permanent retention (RMM 631). Consult PRO on preservation, administration and disposal of long-term (30 years or over) or permanent records still in B/D control (RMM 632).

## Transfer of archival records (RMM 636–639)

Systematically transfer archival records to PRO in accordance with schedules approved by the GRS Director (RMM 636). All government records reaching **30 years old** should be appraised by PRO for archival value (RMM 637). Include supporting documentation or indexes as required (RMM 638).

PRO accepts for permanent preservation only if (RMM 639): the Archivist has confirmed sufficient value; transfer is an appropriate means of preservation and future access; and the records have been prepared as PRO requires.
