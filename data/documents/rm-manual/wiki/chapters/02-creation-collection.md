# Records creation and collection

RMM 300–318: what must be made, and how a complete record is captured. Classification of what has been captured starts at [[03-classification]]. Definition: [[government-records]].

## What to create (RMM 300–304)

Create and collect records to meet operational, policy, legal and financial purposes (RMM 300). Identify business functions and information needs **early**, so records are collected only as necessary (RMM 301).

Examples of what should be created and kept (RMM 302):

- inward and outward communication with external persons and bodies **directly relating** to the organisation’s functions and activities;
- minutes and other records of meetings, consultations and deliberations pertinent to decision-making, policy, procedures or business;
- **major oral decisions and commitments**; and
- individual exercise of discretionary judgement with a **major effect** on functions and activities.

Creation should be **adequate but not excessive**. Collection of personal data must adhere to the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Data Protection Principles in Schedule I) (RMM 303). Shared use should be encouraged; duplication and proliferation of unnecessary records minimised (RMM 304).

## How to create (RMM 310–318)

Recorded information must document government functions, policies, procedures, decisions and transactions **accurately and adequately** (RMM 310). All records, regardless of format and technology, should be **captured into and maintained in an identifiable [[recordkeeping-system]]** (RMM 311).

A captured record should be **complete**: content plus structure plus contextual information, understandable in the organisational process that produced it and in relation to linked records (RMM 312). A complete record should show or link to, as appropriate (RMM 313):

- name of the organisation and person who prepared and transmitted it;
- name of the organisation and/or person it was intended for;
- date and/or time prepared and sent, or received;
- security and sensitivity classification according to content;
- for paper: a file number and title;
- for electronic: links to subject matter, function identification or related records.

Create in the medium and format that facilitates access, use and preservation as required (RMM 314). The basic paper format is the **standard file**; the block of pages should not be thicker than **7 cm**; pages preferably uniform size, aligned, without folded items (RMM 315). **“Post-it” notes and thermal paper should not be used** for adding permanent information (RMM 316). Paper clips on fragile or long-term (30 years or over) documents should have a folded paper sleeve (RMM 317).

Records known to have **permanent value** should be created in a format that permits transfer to and preservation by the [[pro|Public Records Office]] (RMM 318).
