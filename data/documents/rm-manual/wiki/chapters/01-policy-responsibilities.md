# Government records management policy and responsibilities

RMM 200–221 state the government-wide policy and split the work between B/Ds and [[grs]]. DRM duties in full: [[annex-drm-duties]]. Role comparison: [[compare-roles]].

## Policy (RMM 200–201)

It is government policy that each B/D establish a comprehensive programme that will (RMM 200):

- make and keep full and accurate records;
- minimise costs of managing government records;
- provide quality services to users;
- provide necessary security for government information;
- facilitate public access that encourages better understanding of government policies and operations; and
- identify, preserve and provide access to archives of continuing or permanent value.

Responsibilities are **shared** between B/Ds that create and collect records and GRS, the central records management and service agency (RMM 201).

## B/D programme (RMM 206–208)

B/Ds should create, collect and maintain authentic, reliable and usable records as long as required (RMM 206). A comprehensive programme establishes control for creation, organisation, maintenance and disposal (RMM 207). It should cover, among other things: what records to create and at what accuracy/security; form and structure; retrieval and retention; organisation for use; legal and policy compliance; preservation and accessibility over time; retention as long as required and practicable (RMM 208).

## Head of bureau or department (RMM 209)

The [[head-of-bd]], through designated records-management and/or custodian officers, should ensure that:

- a programme is established, maintained and supported by corporate policy with priority and resources;
- records are made to document decisions and transactions and **captured into a [[recordkeeping-system]]**;
- records are created and retained as needed;
- records contain content, structural and contextual information;
- records are authentic, accurate, identifiable, accessible and usable;
- records are protected from unauthorised access, alteration, removal or destruction;
- records of vital and/or historical value are properly protected or preserved; and
- records staff are adequately trained.

## Departmental Records Manager (RMM 210–213)

A [[drm]] should be appointed to assist the head (RMM 210). “Departmental records management programme” means the in-B/D programme that controls creation, organisation, maintenance and disposal (RMM 211).

The DRM is **normally expected to be the Departmental Secretary or an officer holding equivalent position** (RMM 212). The DRM oversees the programme, encourages cooperation in daily handling of records, and coordinates with GRS and other B/Ds. Major duties: Appendix C ([[annex-drm-duties]]).

Newly established B/Ds should forward the DRM’s name, title and contact number to the [[grs-director]]. Any change or replacement should be reported as soon as possible (RMM 213).

## Government Records Service (RMM 220–221)

[[grs]] is the central agency. Main responsibilities (RMM 220): government-wide policy; standards, guidelines and procedures; advisory services and training; records centres, disposal and centralised microfilming; survey, inspection, study and evaluation of departmental programmes; selection, administration and preservation of government archives and valuable publications; access and reference services to archives; **authority** in advising and instructing on administration of records, archives and publications of **long-term (30 years or over) or permanent** value.

B/Ds and GRS should work closely so that government records are effectively managed, operations are accountable, and records of long-term and permanent value that form part of the community’s cultural heritage are protected (RMM 221).
