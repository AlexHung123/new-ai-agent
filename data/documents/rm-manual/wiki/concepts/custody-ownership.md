# Custody and ownership of records

When a function is outsourced, privatised or corporatised, the agreement must specify **custody, ownership and use** of records, which types remain government records, and final disposition — with **prior concurrence of [[grs]]** (RMM 821). **No records** should be included in legislation, a contract of sale, or an agreement transferring a function outside Government unless the [[grs-director]] has issued authority to transfer custody or ownership (RMM 822). Document the agreement and have the GRS Director endorse it (RMM 823).

Appendix B: **custody** is responsibility for care based on physical possession — it does not always include legal ownership or the right to control access. **Ownership** is possession of legal, physical and intellectual rights. **Transfer** is change of physical custody, ownership or responsibility, or moving records from one location to another.

## The distinction (RMM 824–825)

| | Custody transfer | Ownership transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day-to-day management | Moves to the receiving organisation | Moves |
| Physical holding | Receiver | Receiver |
| Legal / intellectual rights | **Remain with Government** | **Relinquished** |
| Still subject to government regulatory requirements | **Yes** | **No** |

RMM 824 is printed as `24.` in the conversion.

## Default on outsourcing

Records arising from outsourced services or contract-supporting services are **normally Government-owned**. The B/D must ensure the contractor meets government recordkeeping requirements (RMM 826). GRS’s role is to keep records needed by Government or important to the community **under Government ownership** (RMM 827). Alternatives to handing over originals: copies; temporary custody with return; access while Government keeps custody (RMM 829). Side-by-side: [[compare-custody-ownership]].
