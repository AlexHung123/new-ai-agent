# Content classification

**Records classification** is the systematic arrangement of records according to a method or system, often by content, to organise related papers and support retrieval (RMM 414). A **classification scheme** is a plan for logical arrangement according to intellectual content, physical formats or other criteria to reflect the programmes and functions of the organisation (RMM 415; Appendix B). Each B/D should maintain a scheme built around its functions that **covers all official records** (RMM 416).

The live fork is [[administrative-records]] (adopt GRS’s subject scheme in Publication No. 3; dispose under GARDS) versus [[programme-records]] (build your own scheme using Publication No. 3 — *Subject Filing*). See [[compare-admin-vs-programme]] and [[03-classification-content]].

## Proper-scheme tests (RMM 420)

Linkages for a continuous record of activities; unique naming and coding over time; security protection and access; vocabulary control; current, comprehensive, logical, consistent, structured.

Keep the scheme current (RMM 421). Maintain an inventory of titles, active/inactive status and locations (RMM 422). Review scheme and inventory regularly (RMM 423). Written procedures for coding, classifying, indexing, retrieving and updating; staff trained (RMM 424). A classification scheme (with indexes) is a **minimum** element of a [[recordkeeping-system]] (RMM 402).

**Security classification** is a different stamp. Do not confuse content codes with Top Secret / Secret / Confidential / Restricted. See [[security-classification]].
