# Vital records

**Vital records** contain information **essential to the continued and effective operation** of a B/D during and after an emergency or disaster (RMM 700; Appendix B). They are not “everything important” and not a synonym for [[archival-records]]. A vital records list and schedule are minimum elements of a [[recordkeeping-system]] (RMM 402). Chapter: [[06-vital-records]]. How-to: Publication No. 6.

## Three categories (RMM 706)

| Category | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Essential | Emergency operating records for conducting emergency services |
| Important | Legal and functional status; financial assets and ownership — needed to re-establish functions |
| Useful | Rights and claims of individuals, organisations, and the public |

The set may be copies, synopses or extracts, on any media (RMM 707). Finding aids are themselves vital (RMM 708). Electronic vital records take their software and contextual documentation with them (RMM 709).

## Selectivity and ownership

Designation **must be highly selective**, by the [[drm]] with approval of the head or deputy head. Choose only what is absolutely essential to proper functioning, or irreplaceable / costly / time-consuming to reconstruct (RMM 710). One of the DRM’s printed duties is to identify and protect vital records ([[annex-drm-duties]] item h).

Protect them, keep readers available, update the set at designated sites, restrict transmittal to authorised personnel, register schedules with GRS if stored there, train, and **test** procedures (RMM 716–729). Time-expired vital records are disposed of under Chapter 6 like any other government record (RMM 730).
