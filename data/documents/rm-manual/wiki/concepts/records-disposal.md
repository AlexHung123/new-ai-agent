# Records disposal

**Disposal does not mean destruction only.** It is the action taken to determine the future of a record when it is no longer in active use: permanent retention; retention for a specified period before destruction; transfer of recorded information into other storage media (microfilm, optical discs); or destroying the records (RMM 601). Appendix B also lists transfer to [[pro]], temporary inactive storage, migration of format, and transfer outside government control.

Appraise against business need, public accountability, regulatory requirements, resources, and public expectation of archival preservation (RMM 600). [[grs]] authorises disposal by approving requests and schedules and issuing authority or agreement (RMM 602). The [[drm]] develops schedules and decides disposal action as approved by the [[head-of-bd]] (RMM 604).

## Hard bars

- **No government records are to be destroyed without the prior agreement of the [[grs-director]]** (RMM 605).
- Unauthorised removal, defacing, alteration, destruction or loss: report to the GRS Director **immediately** (RMM 606).
- Dispose only in accordance with agreed schedules or separate GRS Director approval. Records should not otherwise be destroyed or **removed from the control of the Government** (RMM 627).
- Avoid **ad hoc** disposal (RMM 628). **Alienation** (unlawful transfer or loss of custody) is also a storage-protection duty (RMM 502).

## Paths

See [[compare-disposal-paths]]. Use the [[records-centre]] for inactive records as far as practicable (RMM 629). Records Centre silence-to-destroy: 3 months (RMM 630). Printed materials: Central Preservation Library (RMM 631). Archival value: Archivist of PRO (RMM 626). Time-expired [[vital-records]] follow this chapter (RMM 730).
