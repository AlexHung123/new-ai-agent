# Recordkeeping system

**Recordkeeping** is making and maintaining complete, accurate and reliable evidence of business functions and transactions in the form of recorded information (RMM 118). A **recordkeeping system** is a manual or automated information system in which records are collected, organised and categorised to facilitate retrieval, distribution, use, disposal or preservation (RMM 119, 402). **Recordkeeping requirements** are what a B/D must keep to protect legal, financial and public rights relating to its functions (RMM 120).

All records, regardless of format, should be captured into an identifiable system (RMM 311). Records should be identifiable, retrievable, accessible and available using methods that verify existence and enable them to be found quickly (RMM 400), with unique numbers and/or letters and titles (RMM 401).

## Minimum contents (RMM 402)

- classification scheme(s) with related index(es);
- a complete records inventory;
- records disposal schedules;
- vital records lists; and
- procedures and documentation for coding, filing, retrieval, movement tracking, disposal, and identification and protection of vital records.

When a **new function** is created, a new or expanded system should include at least those same elements (RMM 806). How-to for active files: Publication No. 2.

## Tracking

Maintain an audit trail and the operational origin of records after amalgamation or migration (RMM 480). Document physical movement (RMM 481). Control issue, return to home location, and disposal or transfer — whether manual or barcode (RMM 482).

Only materials of record status (official record copy) belong in the system (RMM 404). See [[compare-records-vs-nonrecords]].
