# Government records

A **government record** is any recorded information or data in any physical format or media created or received by a B/D during its course of official business and kept as **evidence** of policies, decisions, procedures, functions, activities and transactions (RMM 115; Appendix B restates this).

To be considered as evidence, a record must possess **content, structure and context** and be part of a [[recordkeeping-system]] that documents accurately the decision, transaction or activity for which it was created or collected (RMM 116). Content is subject matter. Structure is headings, visual emphasis and other devices that convey meaning. Context is the environment and web of relationships in which the record was raised and used — for example how it relates to others in a group (RMM 117).

## Complete capture

A complete record should show or link to author, addressee, dates sent/received, security classification, and — for paper — file number and title, or — for electronic — links to subject, function or related records (RMM 313). See [[02-creation-collection]].

Examples of what should be created: external communications relating to functions; minutes of decision-making; **major oral decisions and commitments**; individual discretionary judgements with a major effect (RMM 302).

## What is not a government record

[[compare-records-vs-nonrecords|Non-records]] and [[personal-papers]] sit outside the definition. Record status does **not** depend on unique information: extra copies have record status if they document official business **and are kept as evidence** (RMM 405). Materials labelled “personal” but used in official business are official records (RMM 413).

[[electronic-records]] that meet this definition are government records (RMM 123).
