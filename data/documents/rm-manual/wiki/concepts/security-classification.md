# Security classification

Records are classified according to their **level of sensitivity at a particular time** so sensitive information has adequate protection (RMM 430). Four categories (RMM 431): **Top Secret, Secret, Confidential, Restricted**. Definitions and examples live in the **Security Regulations**, not in this Manual (RMM 432).

A document should **not** inherit a grade merely because it refers to classified papers, because previous papers on the subject were so graded, or because of the file it sits on (RMM 433). The printed subject index cites RMM 434; this conversion has no 434 (`source unclear; verify against printed RMM`).

## Time-limited stamps

Impose the restriction for a **stated period** (RMM 440). Consider [[03-classification-security|downgrading]] as soon as special protection is no longer needed (RMM 441). Do not downgrade without reference to the originator and the agency that inherited the originator’s functions (RMM 442). The [[drm]] reviews classified holdings **at least every 5 years** (RMM 443).

## Access and storage overlays

Throughout the life cycle, records are accessible to **authorised users only**, having regard to security classification (RMM 460). Under the Code on Access to Information, **classification is not by itself a valid reason to refuse public access** (RMM 464). See [[records-access]].

Store and protect in ways that reflect security/sensitivity, importance and retention (RMM 503). Classified records also follow the Security Regulations (RMM 504, 452).
