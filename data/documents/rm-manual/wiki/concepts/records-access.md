# Records access

Two public-access tracks sit beside internal retrieval. Chapter: [[03-access]].

## Internal retrieval

Effective means — manuals, forms, index cards or computer-assisted systems — and indexes with enough detail that information can be produced when required (RMM 450–451). Classified transmission follows the Security Regulations (RMM 452). Access throughout the life cycle is to **authorised users only**, having regard to [[security-classification]] (RMM 460).

## Code on Access to Information (current records)

The Code (and the Public Records (Access) Rules) provide public access and set exemptions (RMM 461). Where practicable, identify and describe holdings (except exempted categories) so they can be made accessible **within 10 days** of a written request, **not exceeding 30 days** for more complicated cases (RMM 462). Publish or make available: categories of records; a list of information published or otherwise available; procedures and any charges for information not routinely published (RMM 463).

**A security classification is not by itself a valid reason for refusing public access** under the Code (RMM 464).

## Public Records (Access) Rules (archives)

[[pro]] grants public access to archival records **not less than 30 years old**, or those already published or wholly disclosed (RMM 465). Unclassified archival records reaching 30 years and accessioned by PRO are released **automatically** (RMM 466). Classified ones are reviewed by the creating/responsible B/D; B/Ds should cooperate in the annual review (RMM 467–468). The public may apply for **closed** archival records regardless of grade; the head of the transferring or responsible agency decides (RMM 469). The [[grs-director]] may permit inspection of closed archives, or withhold for preservation (RMM 470–471).

## Personal data

Data subjects access their own personal data under the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance. Other-purpose access by data users needs consent. Exemptions: ss. 51–63 of the Ordinance (RMM 472).
